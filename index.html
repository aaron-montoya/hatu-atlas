    Liora and the Pendant of Thalos

    Open the world map

    The game opens on Silverspring at dawn. Fishing boats are heading out across the bay, and a narrator tells how the village was founded: a faithful few fled the wars of Averra, wrecked their ship on these cliffs, and took the spring, the fish and the wreck as a sign from the divines. The camera drifts along the docks to a small house above the water, and in through an upstairs window. Liora is asleep. Far off, the watchtower horn sounds: monsters have been seen at the treeline again. She doesn't stir.

    Silverspring at a glance

    Silverspring is built up the face of a sea cliff above a sheltered bay, in three tiers joined by wooden stairs and paths cut into the rock. From the water it's a wall of houses; from the clifftop it's a bowl with the sea at the bottom.

    • The docks are at the bottom: boardwalks on stilts threading between boulders at the water's edge, rowboats moored in the gaps, drying nets, and fishing sheds leaning on the cliff. Fishers, boat builders and the net-mender live here.
    • Liora's house is the last building on the docks, right beside the waterfall: a small two-story house on the rock at the foot of the cliff, with its back wall against the cliff face.
    • The market is on a wide rock shelf halfway up. The spring pours out of a cave mouth in the cliff wall into a round stone basin at its center; its stream crosses the market under a little footbridge and falls off the edge as a waterfall into the bay. Houses and stalls ring the basin, including the locked-out man's house with its chimney.
    • The sacred temple stands on a rock point at the seaward end of the market, a round domed hall with seven colored banners, with a sea cave in the rock beneath it that the tide fills. It holds the Divine Pendants.
    • The clifftop is a grassy ridge above everything. The Elder's house stands alone on its hill, and the watchtower is out on the far tip of the headland, reached by a rope bridge.
    • The training yard is a fenced field on the clifftop inside the palisade's east gate, where the ridge meets the forest and the monster sightings have been.
    • The old man's hut sits just outside the east gate, on the road to the Council Fields and the swamp.
    • Ways up and down: the main wooden stair zigzags from the docks to the market; a second stair climbs from the market to the clifftop; and a switchback goat path on the far cliff goes from the beach straight up to the watchtower.

    Wake up, Liora Proposed

    Something taps the shutters, then again, harder. A voice calls up from the street: Wren, Liora's friend and fellow trainee. "Liora! The horn already went. The captain's going to make us run the wall again!" This is the first thing the player controls: press X to get out of bed.

    Liora's room is a short, safe space to learn moving, the camera and X to interact. Nothing in it is required except the door, but most things have something to say:

    • The window opens onto the bay, with the boats heading out and the waterfall roaring beside the house. Wren waves from the boardwalk below and points up the cliff.
    • A shelf holds a little carved owl, worn smooth. Liora doesn't remember where it came from.
    • The rug downstairs has one corner that never lies flat, however many times she kicks it. Players will remember it in Chapter 7, when the hatch under it opens.
    • Breakfast is on the table: a fish bun. Picking it up shows the first item pickup message and heals a quarter heart later, when it matters.
    • The practice sword hangs on a peg by the front door. Take it, and the attack button appears on screen, but there's nothing to hit yet.
    Who else lives in Liora's house? If the house is empty in the morning, players feel her being alone before they know why. If a parent or grandparent is there, they're the natural person to have left the note in Chapter 7, and the house being destroyed hits much harder.

    Through the village

    Wren runs ahead, and the walk to the training yard is the player's first look at Silverspring. It's a straight route, but it passes nearly everything:

    1. Along the docks. Fishers load nets, and the net-mender complains about the monster sightings. Talking is optional; Wren waits at the foot of the big stair and calls "This way!" if Liora wanders off.
    2. Up the stair to the market. The wooden stair zigzags up the cliff, and at the top the market opens out: stalls around the spring basin, the stream running past to the waterfall, and the whole bay below. A man is pacing outside his own front door, locked out. He can't be helped yet; he's the Locked Out side quest later in the chapter.
    3. Past the sacred temple. It stands on its rock point at the end of the market, dome against the sky, doors shut. Wren mentions that the Elder has been praying in there every night for over a week, and nobody knows why.
    4. Up to the clifftop. The second stair climbs past the spring cave to the ridge. The Elder's house is on its hill to one side and the watchtower out on the tip to the other, and the training yard sits straight ahead inside the palisade gate by the treeline. Wren sprints the last stretch. If you follow, the game shows the run button.

    The captain is waiting with crossed arms. "Late again, Liora." Wren gets a look too, for coming back for her.

    Fighter training

    Liora has her wooden practice sword and three hearts. The watch captain runs the young fighters through drills. Each drill is short, and the captain only moves on once you've done it right.

    1. Basic swings. Three straw dummies stand in a row. Press attack to swing; pressing it three times in rhythm chains a three-hit combo (a slash, a backhand slash, then a heavier overhead chop). Knock the head off each dummy.
    2. The spin attack. The captain sets up a ring of five dummies around Liora. Hold attack to charge the sword (it glows and hums), then release to spin and hit everything around her. Clearing the ring in one spin ends the drill.
    3. Lock-on. A dummy swings on a rope from the crossbar. Lock on to it: the camera holds it in frame, Liora faces it and walks in a circle around it instead of running away. While locked on, pressing attack while pushing toward the target makes a forward thrust. Hit the swinging dummy three times.
    4. Dodging. The captain swings a long padded pole in wide sweeps. While locked on, Circle sidesteps left or right and hops backward; when you're not locked on, it's a dodge roll. Get past three sweeps without being hit, then a quick jab from the captain teaches the backhop.
    5. Blocking and parrying. The captain brings the pole down in slow overhead strikes. Hold Triangle and Liora raises her guard: the sword held level in front of her, the flat of the blade braced against her falconry glove. A held block stops the hit but pushes her back a step. Then the captain speeds up, and the drill is timing: press Triangle just as the pole lands, and Liora parries, knocking it aside. The captain staggers, open for a counter. Three parries end the drill.
    6. The sparring match. Wren squares up with a wooden shield. Hitting the front of the shield just clangs off. The trick is to lock on, sidestep around, and strike from the side or behind. Landing three hits wins. This teaches the idea that comes back with the rat soldiers in the first temple: not every enemy can be hit from the front.

    The first real fight Proposed

    The tower horn sounds again, closer. Three Mirelings, low mud creatures with stubby legs and a glowing crack down their backs, crawl over the training yard fence. They charge in straight lines and can't turn mid-charge, so the move is to sidestep or roll as one charges, then hit it while it skids to a stop. Each takes three hits. Small Goins (the currency of Hatu) and the odd heart pop out of them, which introduces pickups.

    When the last Mireling dissolves, the captain hands Liora a real sword, a plain steel blade from the village forge. It replaces the practice sword and does double the damage.

    Sword. Liora's steel sword. Three-hit combo, spin attack (hold), a thrust while locked on, and block and parry on Triangle.
    Liora never carries a shield, because her left arm is the falconry arm and Thalos lands on it. She blocks with the sword itself: blade held level, braced against the glove.
    • Hold Triangle to block. It stops most hits from the front, but heavy attacks like slams and charges push her back or break the guard.
    • Press Triangle as a hit lands to parry. The enemy staggers, open for a combo or a free Thalos dive.
    • A perfect parry, in the last instant, knocks projectiles back. That includes Rovan dives, fireballs and shadow bolts.
    • X jumps. It's the same button as interact: near something she can use, X uses it; anywhere else, X jumps. That's how she clears shockwaves, gaps and low walls.
    The Silverspring smith upgrades the sword once the forge reopens after Chapter 10.

    The Elder's summons

    A messenger, a breathless kid from the market, runs into the yard. The Elder wants Liora at his house, now. The way up the hill is open to explore: villagers chat about the monsters, the net-mender has rumors, and a man in the market square is stuck outside his own front door (the Locked Out side quest, which opens once Liora has the Falconry Glove).

    At the Elder's house, he explains that for nine nights in a row, while praying to the divines, he has heard one name: Liora's. That means the divines are asking her to take up a pendant and become a Pendant Holder. He walks her down to the sacred temple himself.

    The pendant ceremony

    The sacred temple is a round hall with a ring of six stone pedestals, each holding a pendant of colored stone carved with a divine's symbol. In the middle stands a seventh, the largest, holding a pendant carved with an owl. The elders gather at the walls.

    The founders carried seven pendants out of Averra, one for each divine. A Pendant Holder is chosen by one pendant and only ever carries that one; the bond is for life. Liora's is Thalos's. The other six stay on their pedestals, and the game can use them later for the Council's gifts or for other Holders. The colors are yours; the divines and symbols below are proposed.
    • Red: a flame. The Divine of Fire and Courage.
    • Blue: a wave. The Divine of the Sea.
    • Green: a leaf. The Divine of Growth, whose sheaf of grain is on the harvest banners.
    • Yellow: a sun. The Divine of Light.
    • White: a star. The Divine of Stars and Guidance, who shows travelers and sailors the way home.
    • Tan: a mountain. The Divine of Stone and Earth.
    • Purple, in the center: the owl. Thalos, the Divine of Strength.

    The player walks Liora around the ring. As she passes each pendant, it glows faintly and goes dark again. When she reaches the center, the owl pendant lifts off its pedestal on its own and floats into her hands. The elders go silent. The Elder says Thalos's pendant has only ever chosen one bearer before.

    Holding the pendant pulls Liora into a vision. She stands on a dark plain in front of a huge owl made of faint purple light. He is cracked, and three dim spots on his body flicker like dying coals. This is Thalos, the Divine of Strength. He tells her his body was broken into three pieces long ago and sealed in temples across Hatu. Something has gotten into those temples and is making him sick. The creatures in them are like a disease in his body, draining him. He's too weak to come into the world on his own. He asks Liora to free his temples, and tells her that the man who lives past the east gate keeps something that will let him help her.

    Only one person has carried Thalos's pendant before: Selene, the explorer of the Second Age (Chapter 8). The glove the old man keeps was hers, the note under Liora's house in Chapter 7 is hers, and the island where her descendant Maro lives is where she went when Silverspring turned on her. The Elder knows the name but doesn't say it yet.

    The old man's hut

    Past the east gate, on the edge of the road to the fields, is a small hut with a fenced yard, backed up against a low sea bluff. Its owner is Oren, a wiry old man who lives alone with his goat, Turnip, and doesn't get many visitors ("Hm? A visitor? Turnip, we've got a visitor."). He stops at the sight of the pendant. "So it's true. Gran said it would come back up this road one day." He opens a chest and unwraps a worn leather falconer's glove with a purple stone set into its cuff. It belonged to Selene, the last Pendant Holder. His grandmother, Gran Wynn, kept it for her, and when Gran died, he did: "Oiled it every spring since I was a boy, in case." Thalos lives in the stone, and the glove lets him come out into the world as a purple owl.

    Falconry Glove. D-pad up calls Thalos out of the jewel. RB is the Thalos button: tap it to send him at a target, hold it to fly him yourself.

    There's an iron door in the bluff at the back of the yard. Behind it is the Roost: a cave system the sea cut into the bluff long ago, which Gran Wynn fitted out for Selene and her owl. Nobody has been in since the day Gran was buried. It's lit by blue-green glowmoss on the ceilings and glowing shelf mushrooms on the walls, with Gran's old lamps along the walkways, which Oren lights as he goes. Six rooms are joined by short tunnels, and the far end comes out by a stair onto the top of the bluff, above the hut.

    The course comes in two halves. In the first three rooms Oren walks with Liora and teaches sending, and she's on her feet the whole time. As a boy he was one of the village children Gran kept out, and he never got past the rails. At the mural before the flying rooms he stops, because that's as far as he ever got, and he goes round by the path to wait for her on the bench at the top. From there it's just Liora and Thalos. The whole course takes 15 to 20 minutes.

    To Oren, Thalos is just hooting, so now and then he asks "What's he say?", and Liora is the go-between. Liora only ever speaks through a dialogue choice the player makes. Chapter 1 has six choices: what Thalos said, at Oren's door; what to say when Thalos can't remember Selene's name, at the mural; what the Roost was like, and how to thank Oren, on the bench; and two at the waymarker on the road out. None of them changes anything later.

    The Roost

    About 110 by 90 meters end to end, more than four times the area of the old yard plan. The rooms get bigger as the lessons do: the first three are ordinary rooms 6 to 8 meters high, and the Hollow is 50 by 30 meters and 18 meters high. Liora can never reach Thalos's half of any room: each one is split by an old iron rail, 1.8 meters high with spiked tops, which she can't jump or climb, or by a shelf or a drop. That's how every room makes you use the owl.

    How the course is scripted

    • Mostly existing pieces. Strike switches (the gallery bells and the knob at the bottom of the Drop), a hold switch, training dummies, a stone pile and counterweight basket, a fetch item, and the AnimalPen and the goat back outside.
    • New pieces it needs.
      • A door that opens only when three strike switches all fire within a few seconds of each other.
      • Bells that only ring when he's flown into them.
      • A fly-through ring: an Area3D that fires when Thalos passes through it.
      • The little door at the bottom of the Drop that the ring opens.
      • A drawbridge.
      • A winch Liora works with Cross, but only once she's holding the handle.
      • Gran's murals.
    • Two voices. Oren talks Liora through the sending rooms. In the flying rooms Thalos is the only voice. The on-screen toast for each new control shows up the first time it's needed.
    • Optional lines. Here and there Thalos's face icon flashes, and D-pad up plays a line of his own: when the first bell rings, at the first dummy, and before the goat. These are character, not instruction.
    • One thing at a time. Sending is locked until Thalos has been called out once, and flying him is locked until the mural. Every room's exit is the thing that teaches it, so moving on means you've done it. Nothing has a timer except the three chained bells, nothing resets, and a wrong attempt just gets a hint line.
    • Lines that only play if it happens. Each of these has a line that plays only when it happens:
      • Thalos flying head-on into the rock.
      • Flying too far from Liora.
      • Thalos near the hidden Goins.
      • The bar going yellow, and then red.
      • The first time he's recalled tired.
      • Turnip's head-butt.
      • Liora trying to leave the yard mid-lesson ("I've waited my whole life for this. You can give me an hour.").

    Steps

    Sending, with Oren

    1. Call him out. (D-pad up) At the hut. "Gran built a place in the bluff behind this hut, for the woman who wore that glove before you, and her owl. But first things first. He's in the stone. Ask him out." D-pad up. Thalos pours out of the jewel in purple feathers and perches on Liora's raised fist. "...Purple. Gran always said purple. I thought she was having me on." After a couple of seconds he slips back into the stone on his own. "He's not gone. See the stone flickering? That means he wants a word." The face icon flashes on the HUD, and D-pad up plays Thalos's first words in the world: "I know this place. I know this glove." Oren: "Well? What's he say? It's all hooting to me." The player picks Liora's answer:
      • "He knows this place." Oren: "Hah. He remembers."
      • "He knows the glove." Oren: "So he should. I've oiled it every spring since I was a boy."
      • "Hoot." Oren: "Oh, cheeky. You'll get on with my goat."
      He adds that the rest works straight from the stone. He takes a long iron key off a nail, stands holding it a moment ("I haven't turned this key since the day we buried her"), and unlocks the door in the bluff. In the gallery he lights the first of Gran's lamps, and the glowmoss answers along the ceiling. "...They do. Hah."
    2. The first bell. (LT, tap RB) The Bell Gallery is a long, low room, 28 by 14 meters, split down its length by the rail ("older than me, and twice as stubborn"). Four meters past the rail is a bronze bell on a post with an owl carving above it. "You'll not get over that, and you shouldn't. Hold your eye on it." Hold LT locks on. "Now send him." Tap RB. Thalos flies over the rail, rings it, and comes home, and the sound rolls away down the tunnels. "...I used to hear that from my bed, when I was small. Thought it was the sea. That carving. Gran cut those. Wherever you see an owl in the stone, he can do something there." Done when the bell rings.
    3. The chord. (tap RB ×3) Further along, past the rail, three more bells are chained to the gate at the east end of the room, which only lifts if all three ring together. "Gran called it the chord. Not one at a time. Give him all three before he leaves." With no lock-on, a tap sends him to the most centered bell on screen ("You don't have to stare at it. Just look toward one, and tap."). Two more taps while he's still flying queue the other two, and a small purple number floats over each. Send them one at a time and the first has gone quiet before the last: "Send him to the next while he's still out." Done when the gate lifts. "Hah! Still sings, that gate."
    4. Hold the door. (tap RB, then Circle) The Door Room has the rail running corner to corner, a hold switch on the far side (a carved wooden paddle on a stone plinth), and a slab door on Liora's side. "This one he has to sit on. Send him, and you go through while he holds it." Thalos sits on the paddle and the slab grinds up; toast: Liora can move while he's out. Run through and pull the lever just past it (Cross) to keep it up for good. "He'll sit there till you call him. Stand still and call." Circle while standing still calls him back to the glove. "Good. He comes when he's called. Wish I could say the same for my goat." Done when he's back.
    5. Strike. (LT, tap RB) The Training Floor has three straw dummies on a rock shelf three meters up, too high for Liora. "These are for his talons. Same as the bells." Lock on and send him at the first; its head spins round on its peg. "That's what he does to anything with teeth." (Thalos, if asked: "Straw. I had hoped for something that bites back.") Done when it's hit.
    6. Around the board. (LT, lean the right stick, tap RB) The other two dummies have boards in front of them, one open on the right and one on the left. "Not through the board. Around. Hold your eye on it, lean the glove the way you want him to go, and send him." Lock on, lean the right stick toward the open side with Thalos on the glove, and tap RB. Done when both are down. "There. That's every bit of sending I know. All I ever did was watch from this side of the rail." This is the rat soldiers in the first temple.

    The door from the Training Floor opens onto a short tunnel that climbs north. Halfway up, Oren holds his lamp up to the biggest mural in the Roost: a girl standing very still with her eyes shut, and above her an owl in flight, twice her size. "That's her. Gran painted it. She'd stand still as a post and let him do the lot." Then: "Past here's flying. That was between her and the owl. This is as far as I ever got, too." He'll go round by the path and meet her at the top. "Don't rush on my account. Take your time in there, and tell me about it after." When he's gone, Thalos speaks: "This is the part she liked. ...I cannot remember her name. I have been trying since the door." The player picks Liora's answer:

    • "We'll find it." Thalos: "...Yes. We will."
    • "Does it hurt, not remembering?" Thalos: "Like a feather that will not lie flat."
    • Say nothing. Thalos: "...Thank you for not hurrying me."

    He remembers it in Chapter 8, when Maro says "Selene".

    Hold RB to fly Thalos yourself.

    Flying, just Liora and Thalos

    1. The Hollow. (hold RB, the stick, Cross, fly into each bell, Circle) The tunnel comes out onto a stone balcony six meters up the south wall of a cavern 50 meters across and 18 meters high. The ceiling is covered in glowmoss like a night sky, and a still pool fills the middle of the floor. Three bells hang in the dark at different heights: one low over the floor at the west end, one high near the ceiling, and one at middle height out toward the east. "Three bells, hanging in the dark. Her bells. Ring all three and that door opens. You can't reach them from here, and I can't see them from the glove. Fly me. Into each one." A tap does nothing here ("I can't find them on my own from here. Take me: hold the button, and fly me into each bell."). The toasts come one at a time, as each one matters:
      • Hold RB: take control. Thalos launches, and Liora plants her feet. Four seconds into his first flight, once only: "Oh. ...I had forgotten this."
      • Left stick steers; push forward to dip down, pull back to climb.
      • Cross flaps to climb higher. The high bell needs it, and the stamina bar fades in for the first time under the hearts.
      • Fly him into each bell, with a counter: Bells: 1 / 3.
      • Tap Circle gives control back to Liora, and he keeps flying along with her. Hold Circle calls him all the way back.
      Nothing hurries this. The Hollow is the place to just fly: over the pool he skims the surface and can't go under. Into the rock head-on and he's knocked down, then hops and takes off again ("Slower. I would like to keep my beak."). Too far from Liora and the screen edge pulses ("Not so far. I lose you past here."). There are Goins up on a ledge ("Somebody hid them for the owl. Somebody thought I would come back."). When the third bell rings, the door at the end of the balcony opens. "There. Her bells, all three. I think she would have laughed. Now call me home: hold Circle, wherever I am." Done when he's back on the glove.
    2. The Drop: hover, shadow, drop. (LT, RT) Through the door is the rim of a round shaft 14 meters across and 26 meters deep. The bridge across it is up, and its chain runs all the way down to the bottom. A stone ring hangs flat on a bracket in the middle of the shaft, and on the floor under it is a pale patch of moss. "There's a ring in the middle, on a bracket. Fly me out over it and stop. Watch my shadow: when it's on the moss under the ring, I'll drop straight down through it." LT: he stops and hovers, and the stick turns him. Line his shadow up on the moss, then, still hovering, hold RT: he drops straight down through the ring. Let go of RT to pull out. Done when he's through.
    3. Land and hop. (the stick, RB) Going through the ring opens a little wooden door at the bottom of the shaft, where the chain goes into the wall. "Too low to fly through: land me, and hop me in." Fly down onto the floor and he lands; the stick hops him through the little door, and RB on the knob inside brings the bridge creaking down across the shaft at Liora's feet. Done when the bridge is down.
    4. The climb out. (stamina) "Out's a long climb. Rest on the ledges on the way, or call me home: the glove's the quickest rest there is." Rock ledges stick out of the shaft wall on the way up; land on one and he sits, and the bar fills back up. When the bar goes yellow: "I am out of practice, Liora. Let me sit a moment." Push on with an empty bar and he can't climb, and a hover just sinks him: "Nothing left. Put me down. Please." Hold Circle works at any point too. The first time he's recalled tired: "The glove is warm. She kept it warm, too." Done when he's back with Liora, however he got there.
    5. Carry and drop. (RB, Triangle) Across the bridge is the Storeroom, a high, square room of old shelves. The rail leaves Liora a narrow strip along the west wall, and a grate across her strip blocks the way on. "That basket up there holds the grate down. Weigh it and the grate lifts. Fly me to the stone heap and I'll pick one up. Then hover me over the basket, and let go when my shadow's in it. Three should do." Fly Thalos over the stone heap and RB picks one up. Hover over the wicker basket, five meters up on his side, and Triangle drops it onto his shadow. A miss lies on the floor and can be picked up again. Done when three stones are in and the grate is up.
    6. Bring it to her. (RB, fly past Liora) Past the grate, the exit door has a winch beside it. Liora tries it and it spins loose: The winch has no handle. "She kept it on the top shelf, so the village children couldn't get in. Fly me up for it, then fly past you. You take it out of my talons." Fly up to the top shelf, eight meters up the east wall; RB takes the iron handle. Fly him past Liora to hand it over, then Cross at the winch fits it and winds the door up onto a stair. Done when the door is open.

    The stair climbs about forty steps in the rock. "Tap for me with nothing to see, and I'll come with you. I would rather fly than sit in the stone." An empty RB tap and he flies out and follows her: off her shoulder while she walks, ahead when she runs.

    The bench

    The trapdoor opens into daylight on top of the bluff, with the whole bay below and the cliffs of Silverspring off to the west. Oren is on a bench by a little stone owl, looking out over the water, and he doesn't turn round straight away. "There you are. Up the stair, all forty of them. I came round by the path. Took me longer than you, I expect." Then: "Well? I never saw past that mural, you know. Not once. What's it like in there?" The player picks Liora's answer:

    • "The ceiling glows like the night sky." Oren: "...Gran said that. Word for word. I always thought she was making it pretty for me."
    • "The bells still ring." Oren: "I know. I heard them up here, through the rock. First time since I was small."
    • "We found a handle on the top shelf." Oren laughs: "That'd be my fault. Gran put it up there so the village children couldn't get in. I was the village children."

    He tells her that Gran ran the Roost for the woman who carried the glove before her, that the woman went off to an island when the village turned on her, and that she said the owl would choose again one day. "Gran believed her. I believed Gran. Nobody else did. ...Seems we were right." He doesn't know her name; Maro does, in Chapter 8. He gives Liora a sack of apples for the road, and the Roost's key: "The Roost's yours now, yours and his. Come back and ring the bells now and then, so I know you're all right."

    A sack of Apples. They're in the Satchel: hold L1 (Tab), press R1 (E), and let go on them to eat one.

    Thalos: "She sat up here too. I remember the wind. Liora. Thank him for me." The player picks how:

    • "He says thank you." Oren: "...Does he. Well. Tell him he's welcome. Tell him he took his time."
    • "Thank him yourself." Thalos hops off the glove onto the bench and bows his head to Oren. "...Oh. Well. You're welcome. You took your time."

    The Storm Courtyard is a round stone yard on the outside of the tree, high above the swamp, under a thunderstorm that never stops. Rain pours down and puddles cover the stones. A knee-deep pool sits in the middle, and drain channels run from it out to two of the bigger puddles.     The Gloomhorn is a huge, bull-shouldered swamp beast with a crown of black horns. It's invisible in the rain. Any lightning strike makes it fade into view almost instantly; it stays solid for about a second and a half, then fades out again over the next second and a half. Lock-on only works while it can be seen.

    How to read it: between flashes, watch and listen. Before every charge it paws the stone and snorts out a puff of steam, and the puddles splash where it stands. Then it drops its head and charges in a straight line at the spot where Liora is standing. The ground rumbles as it comes.

    Calling lightning: lock on to a tower and tap the Thalos button. He strikes the copper plate, and lightning hits that tower a second later, showing the Gloomhorn on demand. Each call costs Thalos stamina, so call it when the natural lightning won't come in time.

    Dodging: the charge can't be blocked. Roll or sidestep out of its path at the last moment. A late dodge, in the final instant before it hits, slows time for a moment and makes it overshoot further. If Liora stays close to it, it hooks at her with its claws instead. Those can be parried with Triangle.

    Phase 1, The Charges: natural lightning strikes every six seconds or so. Stand with a tower at your back and wait for the charge, then dodge late. If it misses into open ground, it skids, turns and comes again. If it slams into a tower or the wall, it's dazed for three seconds. Run in and hit it. If it ever crashes into the cracked tower, the tower topples into the pool and Phase 2 starts early. If it hasn't happened after two crashes, the Gloomhorn wrenches the tower down itself.

    Phase 2, The Pool: the fallen tower lies across the pool with its copper plate sticking out of the water. Now the Gloomhorn winds up faster and charges twice in a row, skidding and coming straight back without stopping. Wading through the pool slows it down, so stand on the far side, where its charge has to cross the water. Have Thalos strike the fallen plate just before it reaches the middle. Lightning fills the pool with electricity, which runs along the drain channels into the linked puddles, and the Gloomhorn is stunned for four seconds, the longest opening in the fight. Don't be standing in the water or a linked puddle yourself. Two pool shocks end the phase.

    Phase 3, Blackout: the storm goes dark and natural lightning stops. The only flashes now are the ones Thalos calls, so his stamina matters. The Gloomhorn charges faster and doesn't stop after a miss; it loops around and charges again.     There's also a longer route that needs only one snowball, through the east side of the room and back along row 5 against the pillar at G5. Either works; it's there for players who like to experiment.

    Thalos can place all three snowballs before Liora moves, as long as she's quick about the slides. If a plan goes wrong, she can always slide back to the stone strip and start over, and old snowballs melt on their own.

    4Frigidu Den P4

    Unlock the hub's east door. This room is a cave of ice about 18 by 22 meters. A raised ledge of bare stone runs along its east side, three steps up, about four meters wide. The rest of the floor is old blue ice. Two things stand out: a doorway in the north wall sealed by a wall of ice a meter thick, with the orange glow of a forge behind it, and an open archway in the east wall that lets in wind and a whistling sound.

    The pack. Five Frigidu and a big one with thicker armor drop from the ceiling as Liora walks in. On the ice, a Frigidu whose armor is broken grows it back in about four seconds. On the stone ledge, it can't. Liora could fight them on the stone, but they won't follow her there, because they don't like it. They follow Thalos, though:

    1. Hold RB and fly Thalos at the pack, then tap Circle. Control comes back to Liora, and Thalos stays out pestering the nearest Frigidu, circling its face. The whole pack starts chasing him.
    2. Hold RB again and fly him low and slow over the stone ledge. Like Turnip in the yard, they follow him while he's low and close, and they scramble up the steps after him.
    3. Tap Circle again to leave him pestering them on the stone, and fight them there with Liora. Armor broken on the stone stays broken.

    Pestering costs stamina, and when he runs out, Thalos stops and the pack wanders back toward the ice. So take them in two groups, and recall him to the glove in between. The big one takes two full combos to break its armor, so fight it last, on the stone, and only when Thalos's bar is full.

    Dungeon Map

    When the pack is gone, a chest drops out of a crack in the ceiling onto the ledge. The Map shows the ice wall to the north, and the forge behind it, glowing. Sword hits barely scratch the wall. The way on is the east archway.

    5Wind Gallery P5

    The east archway opens onto a long gallery cut along the outside face of the mountain, about 40 meters from end to end, running north. The east wall is three tall arched windows open to the sky, with a stone buttress between each pair. The wind comes through the windows in gusts, one window after another, so a gust rolls down the gallery like a wave. The middle of the floor fell into the mountain long ago, leaving a chasm 30 meters long that Liora can't cross. At the far north end, past the chasm, is a stone landing with a chest and a locked door, and the drawbridge that would cross the chasm is raised against the landing.

    The bridge's switch is on the far landing, and a drift of snow has buried it. Only Thalos can get there, and this room is where the game teaches him to move through gusts properly, with room to fail:

    • Windbreaks. Behind each buttress is a pocket of still air. Fly Thalos to one, hold LT to hover in its shelter, let the gust howl past, then fly on to the next.
    • Shutters. Each window has a pair of wooden shutters on an iron hinge, driven by a wind wheel above the window. Hover in front of a wheel and press RT: the wing blast spins it, and the shutters swing shut for about ten seconds. With that window shut, its stretch of the gallery is safe. A player who works this out can shut a window ahead and glide past it, instead of waiting behind every buttress.
    • Stamina. Hovering costs stamina, and three long hovers will run the bar into yellow. The tops of the buttresses are flat. Land on one, on its sheltered side, and Thalos sits and rests out of the wind.

    At the far end, hover in front of the drift, wing-blast the snow away, and strike the owl switch underneath with RB, or fly straight into it. The drawbridge comes down with a crash across the chasm. Liora crosses. Two Frigidu are waiting on the landing, and here they're on stone, so their armor stays broken.

    Small Key 2

    The locked door on the landing is the only one in reach. Unlock it.

    6Icicle Shaft P6

    Beyond the door is a tall, narrow shaft of ice, about 30 meters high and six across. The air gets warmer the higher Liora climbs, which is strange this close to the summit. The way on is straight up, on the Ice Picks.

    The icicles. At about 8, 16 and 24 meters, a rib of rock juts out across the shaft, and each rib is hung with a band of icicles. The climbable ice runs right up under each rib. Climbing under a band makes it shake loose and fall on Liora, for one heart and a fall back to the last ledge. So before each band, stop on a rest ledge, hold RB and fly Thalos up the shaft ahead of her. The rest ledges are cut into the shaft's side walls, tucked in under overhangs and out of the fall line, so the ice comes down past Liora and shatters on the floor far below. Each cluster gets a purple outline when he's close: RB, or flying into it, knocks it down. Flying Thalos means Liora isn't climbing, and her climbing stamina isn't draining, so it's always done from a ledge, never from the wall.

    Not every icicle is in the way of the climb. Some hang down in front of the wall like a curtain, hiding a stretch of climbable ice behind them. Above the second band, one curtain hides a sideways route across the wall to a ledge in a notch. Knock it down with Thalos and the route shows, a strip of rough, pick-scarred ice running off to the side.

    On a side ledge in a notch two-thirds of the way up the Icicle Shaft, behind a curtain of icicles. Knock the curtain down with Thalos, then climb sideways across the wall to reach it.

    The damper. At the top, a big stone chimney passes up through the shaft. It's the flue from the temple's forge. The Frostmurians didn't burn coal: they built the forge over a vent in the mountain where hot air rises from deep below, and the flue carries it out. Its iron damper has been stuck wide open for years, so all that heat goes straight up and out of the peak, and the forge below has frozen over. That's why the top of the shaft is warm. The damper's lever has an owl carving; send Thalos to it with a tap of RB. The damper clanks shut, and the warmth in the shaft fades. With nowhere to go, the heat backs up into the forge, and far below there's a groan of ice and the sound of running water.

    The way down. Beside the flue, an iron grate held by a bolt on this side covers the top of an old ice chute. Slide the bolt (Cross) and jump in. The chute runs back west, curving down around the outside of the flue and over the forge, and spits Liora out through a hole in the Den's ceiling into the big snowdrift in its north corner, a short drop. It's one-way: the hole is too high to reach from below. She lands right beside the ice wall, which is already sagging and streaming with water.

    7Rimeforge P7 Mini-boss

    Within a minute the wall slumps into a steaming puddle, and there's an open doorway. The room beyond is the old Frostmurian forge, about 20 meters across: anvils, iron grates in the floor over the heat vent, glowing faintly now and breathing hot air, racks of iron tools, and a massive figure standing in the middle, crusted in ice. The room is warm for the first time in the temple, and water drips from the ceiling.

    The Rimeforged Sentinel is an iron golem the Frostmurians built to guard their forge, now armored in ice so thick that the warmth has barely started it dripping. Sword hits bounce off it. The golem has a boiler inside it, and the heat from the vent has woken it up.

    • Attacks: slow, heavy punches, and a two-handed slam that sends a shockwave across the floor; jump or roll through it. The slam buries both its fists in the floor for about three seconds.
    • The valve: between its shoulders is the boiler's big steam valve, with an owl carving. It's always on its back, away from Liora. Lock on to the Sentinel itself: a straight send hits its iced-up chest and glances off. Lock on, lean the right stick left or right with Thalos on the glove, and tap RB. Thalos swings round behind it and strikes the valve. Steam blasts out of its joints and melts a patch of its ice armor, and the bare iron glows. Hit the bare patch.
    • Phase 2: with half its ice gone, it gets faster and starts sweeping its arms in a spin. Get out of reach during the spin. Then bait the slam by standing at middle distance, about four meters away, and repeat the flank send. Three vented patches and it falls.

    The Sentinel collapses. Its right hand is a huge iron gauntlet, and it comes loose and drops onto the anvil, shrinking to Liora's size with a glow of old magic.

    Magnetic Gauntlet. Pull or push anything made of iron. Also pull Liora toward heavy iron anchors.

    8Magnet Hall P8

    North of the forge is a long hall built to teach the gauntlet, one thing per section, with an iron gate between each section and the next:

    1. Pull. An iron crate sits across the room, and a pressure plate lies on the floor halfway between it and Liora. Aim the gauntlet at the crate and pull: it slides across the floor toward her, and stops whenever she lets go. A pulled crate always stops a step in front of her, never on her. Let go when it's on the plate. If it overshoots, push it back a little. The first gate opens.
    2. Push. A square pit a crate's width and a crate's depth blocks the way, with a crate on Liora's side. Push the crate into it. It drops in and fills it level, and Liora walks across.
    3. Anchor. A big iron ring is bolted high on the wall on the far side of a chasm. It's too heavy to move, so when Liora pulls on it, she flies toward it instead, and lands on the ledge below it. This is the rule for the rest of the game: pull something lighter than Liora and it comes to her; pull something heavier and she goes to it.
    4. Too heavy for Thalos. The last chest is inside an iron cage on a shelf four meters up. The cage has an owl carving, so a tap of RB sends Thalos at it: he grabs the cage, flaps hard and can't lift it, then lets go and comes home. It's a scripted reaction for this one cage, and it's on purpose, and it sets up Talon Grip at the end of the temple. Liora pulls the whole cage off the shelf with the gauntlet. It crashes open on the floor.
    Small Key 3

    There's no going back the way she came: the anchor ring only pulls one way across the chasm. So the hall's far end has a gate that opens from this side onto a narrow side passage, which runs back south along the hall's outer wall to the forge. From the forge, the Den and the east door lead back to the hub.

    9Hall of Offerings, again P2

    Back in the central hall, the magnetic seal on the north door turns out to be just a big iron disk. Lock on and pull with the gauntlet. It grinds out of its socket a little at a time, and the door swings open with a deep boom that shakes snow out of the ice dome.

    10Pendulum Tower P9

    The north door opens into a round tower, about 14 meters across and 30 meters high, open to the sky at the top, where snow sifts down. Most of its wall is old black ice, so hard and smooth that the Ice Picks skate off it. Ice Picks only bite in three strips of rough white ice, and the strips don't line up. The first runs from the floor up the south wall, beside the door, to a ledge at 9 meters. The second runs up the north wall from 9 to a ledge at 18. The third runs up the south wall again, from 18 to a narrow perch at about 27. The way out is the top ledge on the north side at the same height, a doorway onto the mountain. So Liora has to cross the tower three times.

    The Frostmurians hung a pendulum at each crossing: three iron pendulums, each on a short chain from an iron beam a few meters above where it's needed, each swinging across the tower from one side to the other. The third one carries her from the perch to the top ledge. Each one is a two-meter iron bob with a ring on each side. All three are frozen still, hanging near the wall, held by ice at their pivots.

    1. Climb. Climb the first strip to the first ledge.
    2. Free it. Pull on the first bob with the gauntlet. It's lighter than the beam but heavier than Liora, so the pull cracks the ice at its pivot and sets it swinging, and on the backswing it comes toward her ledge.
    3. Ride it. As it swings in close, pull on its ring. She flies to it and clings on, and it carries her across the tower.
    4. Jump off. At the far end of the swing, there's a moment where it stops dead before swinging back. Jump off with X there and catch the next strip with the Ice Picks. Climb to the next ledge and do it again with the next pendulum. Off the third pendulum, she jumps onto the top ledge instead of a wall.

    Thalos's part. The second strip, the one Liora has to catch off the first pendulum, is caked in snow, and Ice Picks don't bite in snow. From the first ledge, hold RB, fly Thalos across, hover, and wing-blast it clear first. Under the top ledge, a fringe of icicles hangs from the lip; Thalos knocks them down so Liora isn't climbing up into them.

    If Liora misses a catch, she falls. That's half a heart, and she's back on the last ledge she stood on, with the pendulum still swinging for another try.

    11Spirit Bridge P10

    The top of the tower opens onto the outside of the mountain, onto a ledge above a chasm 24 meters wide, with the summit above it all. A blizzard blows across the gap. An old iron frame arches over the gap, and five iron plates hang from it on chains, swinging in the wind. Lined up, they'd make a bridge.

    The plates. Pull the first plate into line with the gauntlet. It locks against a hook for about three seconds, then the wind pushes it loose again. There's no pulling all five in a row in time.

    The anchor switch. On the far side of the chasm is an anchor switch with an owl carving that locks every plate in place at once. It's buried in a drift. Thalos has to get there, across the open chasm, with a gust every five seconds and only three spires of rock rising out of the chasm on the way, each big enough to shelter an owl. It's the Wind Gallery's lesson with no shutters:

    1. After a gust, fly to the first spire and hover in its shelter, on the side away from the wind.
    2. Fly on to the second. It has a little ledge on its sheltered side, the place to land and rest; by now the bar is yellow.
    3. Fly on to the third, then to the drift.
    4. Hover in front of the drift, wing-blast the snow away, and strike the switch, all inside the five seconds between gusts.

    When Thalos hits the switch, all five plates swing into line and lock with a clang. Cross.

    On the far side, the boss door is to the north, a round door of blue ice with a seal shaped like a snowflake. It needs the Frost Sigil. A locked door leads west, and it's the only one in reach.

    12Rail Junction P11

    Unlock the west door with Small Key 3. This is the Frostmurians' old ore yard: a big icy cavern with iron mine carts on iron rails. The rails start at a loading platform on the south side, run north through three switch points, and branch out to dead ends and the vault door at the far end. A stone slate by the platform has the yard's route painted on it: from the vault door back to the platform, the line goes left at the first switch, straight at the second, and right at the third.

    Each switch point has an owl carving and a little iron flag that shows which way it's set, and striking a switch flips it. Right now:

    • Switch 1 is set straight. It needs flipping to the left.
    • Switch 2 is set to turn. It needs flipping to straight.
    • Switch 3 is set to the left, and it needs flipping to the right. It's around the back of a big ore heap, out of sight from the platform.

    The run. Climb into the cart on the platform.

    1. From the cart, lock on to Switch 1 and tap RB, then tap again on Switch 2 to queue it. Thalos flips both and comes home.
    2. A magnet post stands beside the track at the end of the first straight, where the track tips down a slope. Pull on it with the gauntlet. The post is heavier than Liora and the cart together, so the pull drags the cart along the rails toward it. Let go as the cart passes it, and the cart rolls on down the slope, picking up speed.
    3. Switch 3 can't be seen from the cart until it's too late. As soon as the cart is moving, hold RB and fly Thalos ahead around the ore heap, and strike Switch 3 before the cart gets there. From the post, the cart takes about twelve seconds to reach it. Liora stays in the cart while you fly; nothing in here attacks her.

    Get a switch wrong and the cart ends up in a dead end against a buffer. Every dead end has a straight line of rails back to a magnet post by the platform, so pulling on that post drags the cart back up. The dead end off Switch 2 has a small chest of Goins, to make the mistake worth something.

    13Frost Sigil Vault P12

    The right track ends at the vault, a small apse-shaped room behind an iron door that opens as the cart hits the buffer. There's no chest in sight, but in one wall, behind a sheet of clear ice about a hand thick, is a dark shape. The chest is in there, in a niche. It's iron-bound and lighter than Liora, so lock on and pull it out with the gauntlet. It cracks through the ice sheet and slides out across the floor to her feet.

    Frost Sigil. An ice-blue seal shaped like a snowflake, which opens the Summit Shrine.

    A second cart sits on a return track that runs straight back to the platform. Climb in and pull on the platform's magnet post to ride it back, then cross the Spirit Bridge to the boss door. The plates are still locked.

    14Summit Shrine P13 Boss

    The Frost Sigil opens the door to the very top of the mountain, a round shrine open to the sky, about 24 meters across, with sheer walls of rough white ice about 20 meters high. One wall is carved with a huge Frostmurian figure. At its center, on a pedestal, the piece of Thalos glows: a fragment of purple light. Thalos cries out.

    Then a figure in a dark hooded cloak steps out from behind the pedestal and takes the piece. This is Liora's first look at Varyon. He doesn't fight her. He looks at the pendant, and at the owl on her arm, and seems almost amused. He presses his hand to the shrine's wall, where the carved figure is, and says something in a language Liora doesn't know. Then he's gone, in a swirl of black smoke.

    The carving moves. The ice cracks, and out steps Kolvagar, Spirit of the Peak: a colossal ancestral Frostmurian spirit, twice as tall as the Sentinel, wearing armor made of iron plates bound with chains. It's the giant from the offering bowls.

    Phase 1: the armor. Kolvagar stomps toward Liora and swings huge fists. Each iron plate of his armor has a ring on it, and each plate is only lashed on with chain. A plate is lighter than Liora, so lock on to a ring and pull with the gauntlet, and it tears loose and flies past her. He staggers each time, and the bare spot glows. Hit the bare spot. He wears six plates. Thalos can strike a bare spot too, for a point of damage, while Liora lines up the next ring.

    Phase 2: the walls. With his armor gone, he leaps onto the shrine's ice walls and climbs out of reach, tearing chunks of ice off the wall and hurling them down. While he's throwing, Liora can't climb near him. So hold RB, fly Thalos up to him, and tap Circle. Thalos stays and pesters him, circling his face, and Kolvagar stops throwing to swat at the owl. That lasts until Thalos's stamina runs out, so it's a timer: climb after Kolvagar with the Ice Picks while the bar drains, get up beside him, and strike his hands. He falls back to the floor, stunned. Do it twice. Start each climb with a full bar.

    Phase 3: the chest strap. He climbs again, this time to the very top, too high to climb to in time. An iron chain is wrapped around his chest like a strap. He's far heavier than Liora, so when she locks on to the chain and pulls, it's the anchor rule from the Magnet Hall: she's yanked up the wall to him. She lands on his chest, hanging from the chain. Strike the chain's lock, a big iron clasp over his heart. It snaps, the chain unwinds, and Liora drops away to the snow on the floor as Kolvagar loses his grip and comes crashing down after her. His chest cracks open on the impact, showing a blue glowing core. Strike it.

    Kolvagar slowly turns to snow and blows away. The Frostmurians will later say his spirit went back to the peak, freed of whatever Varyon did to him.

    Great Totem of Strength. Where Kolvagar crashed, a full-size owl totem rises out of the snow, glowing gold. Touch it for one full extra heart. Every temple boss leaves one.
    Talon Grip. Thalos can lift heavy things, as heavy as Liora could carry, and fly with them. That includes the iron cage he couldn't lift in the Magnet Hall. If you send him at a locked-on target while he's carrying something, he flies there and hovers over it with the load until you recall him. Every scroll also gives Thalos 25% more stamina. See all scrolls.

    The pedestal is empty. Thalos is quiet for a long time. Then he says the man has two of his pieces now. There's only one left, hidden in the desert where no one goes, and Liora won't find it without help. He knows where that help is: at home, in Silverspring.

    Varyon has two pieces of Thalos. The last one is hidden in the desert, where no one goes. Thalos can't remember much about the one who carried him before, but he remembers that she left something in Silverspring for whoever he chose next, and he thinks they'll need it. The way home is down the Gorge to the Reed Delta, where the Rocklets have moored a raft for her. It rides the river all the way down past the fields to Silverspring's bay. From now on, the raft is a one-way shortcut home.

    Smoke on the coast

    The raft comes round the last bend of the river into the bay, and Silverspring comes into view up on its cliffs. There's smoke. The village is mostly destroyed. The watchtower has collapsed and its rope bridge hangs in two halves down the cliff, half the houses are burnt out, and the docks are smashed. Boats are sunk at their moorings. The music cuts out. The raft runs aground on the shingle below the docks, and Liora climbs up through the wreckage.

    Who destroyed Silverspring? If it was the monsters from the treeline, the threat feels like it's growing as Varyon gets stronger. If it was Varyon himself, or monsters he sent after the pendant, it becomes personal. Survivors could describe what they saw.

    The bell

    Oren's hut is past the east gate, and the road out to it is scorched. If Liora opens the Roost and has Thalos ring the gallery bell the way she has all game, it rings, and the echo runs away down the tunnels, and nobody calls down to her. No line, no music; just the bell dying away and the sea. Thalos's face doesn't flash. It's an optional beat, so only the players who kept ringing it will feel it, and they'll feel it most.

    The hut itself Proposed: the door is hanging open and the chest where the glove was kept is overturned. Turnip is in her pen, alone. When Thalos comes out she doesn't chase him. She just watches.

    What happened to Oren? The silence works whatever the answer, but the answer decides what it means. One option: he's alive, found later. When the attack came he took the village children into the Roost, the one place in Silverspring with an iron door and rails nothing can get past, and hid them in the Storeroom, too deep to hear the bell. Liora finds him there in this chapter or a later one ("Gran kept the village children out. I let them in."), and after that the bell gets answered again. The other: he's gone, and the bell stays silent for the rest of the game.

    Survivors Proposed

    The survivors have taken shelter in the sacred temple, the one building still standing. The pendants are untouched. The Elder is hurt but alive, and he's glad to see Liora. A few villagers are still missing, trapped in the wreckage around town, and this is where the Magnetic Gauntlet gets its first use outside a temple:

    1. The forge. The smith is pinned under a collapsed rack of iron bars and tongs. Each piece is lighter than Liora, so the gauntlet pulls them off him one at a time, while he tells her which ones are holding the rest up.
    2. The net-mender's house. The doorway is blocked by her little iron cook-stove, which fell over when the wall came down. It's lighter than Liora, so pull it out of the doorway.
    3. The market. A child is trapped in a cellar under the collapsed market stalls, too frightened to move in the dark. The gap into it is too small for Liora. Hold RB and fly Thalos down through it. The child sees the owl, follows him through the rubble to the gap, and squeezes out after him.

    Each rescued villager goes to the temple. Some of them will have quests or shops for the rest of the game.

    Is the watch captain among the survivors? The captain trained Liora and gave her the sword. If they're missing, finding them later is a strong emotional thread.

    Liora's house

    Liora's own house is half-collapsed: the upper floor and the front wall have burned and fallen in, but the back room, against the cliff face, is still standing. The fire cracked the old stone hearth in the back room, and a stone has fallen out of it. In the gap behind it is a packet wrapped in oilcloth: a very old folded note, addressed to whoever the owl chooses next. It talks about a hidden room under this house, and says that if the owl ever calls you, what's down there is yours.

    Selene. One branch of her family stayed in Silverspring, and Liora is from it. Selene left the necklace with them so that whoever the owl chose next could reach her island, hid the note in their hearth, and the family forgot it was there. The note is in her hand. It isn't signed, so Thalos still doesn't get her name back here; that waits for Maro.

    The back room's floor is the rock itself. Pull the rug aside, and there's an iron hatch set flush into it, with no handle or ring, nothing to get fingers under. It's the gauntlet that opens it: pull, and it swings up on its hinge. A ladder leads down into a room cut into the rock.

    The basement is a small stone room full of old things: a sea chest, a coil of rope, faded charts of the coast, and a model of the founders' ship. Inside the sea chest, wrapped in sailcloth, is a necklace of white and pink shells strung on silver wire. When Liora touches it, the sound of the sea fills the room.

    Seashell Necklace. Part bodies of water. Press R2 to turn it on: walk into a lake or the sea, and the water pulls away into walls around Liora so she can walk on the bottom, closing again behind her. Press R2 again to turn it off and swim normally.
    In the basement, hiding behind the sea chest. That's ten; bring them to Mother Firefly for the second upgrade, which lets fireflies be drawn in from farther away.

    Trying out the necklace

    Thalos suggests trying the necklace in the bay. Walk out along what's left of the docks and straight off the end of the boardwalk into the water. Instead of swimming, Liora keeps walking, and the sea pulls back from her in a circle, rising into walls of water, with foam at the top. Fish flop on the sand. Turn around, and the path behind her closes with a crash.

    Walk under the temple's rock point. With the water parted, the sea cave beneath it is dry for the first time: tide pools, barnacled steps and a small cave at the back. Inside, the lantern pulses.

    In the sea cave under the temple's rock point. Only reachable with the water parted.

    Further out, just at the edge of where the sea can be parted, Liora can see the wreck of the founders' ship lying on the sea floor. It's a nice place to put lore later.

    From the beach, Thalos looks out at a small island off the coast, with a lighthouse on it, and goes quiet. He's been there before, he says, a long time ago. Whatever she left for him, the rest of it is out there.

    Selene was a Silverspring explorer in the Second Age, and the only person before Liora to carry the Pendant of Thalos. She went against the elders' wishes, set out on expeditions with Thalos on her arm, found the desert temple, and helped the robots who crashed beside it. The village shunned her for it, and she moved out to a small island off the coast, leaving the glove and the necklace behind for whoever Thalos chose next.     Crossing the strait

    This is the Seashell Necklace's big set piece. From the rocky point a few minutes down the Shore south of Silverspring, Liora walks into the sea, and keeps walking. The water rises higher and higher on either side as the seabed drops away, until the walls tower over her and the light turns blue-green.

    • Fish swim inside the walls, and if Liora gets close, they press against the edge of the water to look at her.
    • Partway across, a huge dark shape glides through the wall on one side, keeping pace with Liora. It never attacks, but the music goes tense.
    • Tidecrabs skitter across the sea floor: big armored crabs, too heavy for Thalos to move before now. With Talon Grip, send him at one and he lifts it and drops it on its back, legs waving. Hit its soft underside before it rights itself. This is Talon Grip's first real use.
    • Anchors, rope and old cargo from the founders' wreck lie on the sea floor, half buried in sand.
    What's the shape in the water? A sea serpent that comes back as a boss, a friendly giant that shows up again, or just atmosphere.

    Reaching the fortress Proposed

    Far off Hatu's western coast, where the sea is deepest, a single crag of black rock rises out of the water and bends over into a huge natural arch. Varyon built his fortress along the top of it: towers, chimneys and a long hall made from the hulls of Averran warships, all bolted to the ridge. At the arch's highest point, a thin spire climbs far above everything else.

    It's always night here. The Obsidian Heart holds the fortress in permanent darkness, under an enormous full moon that hangs low behind the spire, bigger than anywhere else in the game. The sky fades from deep violet overhead to a dull amber glow along the horizon, and tattered clouds streak across the moon.

    For most of the game, no one can even see the crag. A storm sits over the deepest water, and from the coast it looks like an ordinary squall that never moves. When Varyon takes the Obsidian Heart in Chapter 12, the storm breaks for the first time, and from Silverspring's cliffs the arch, the spire and the enormous moon are suddenly there on the horizon.

    There's no bridge. Liora walks there across the sea floor with the Seashell Necklace, through the deepest water in the game. The walls of water rise so high they block out the sky, and the shape from the strait swims past again. She surfaces at the foot of the crag, where jagged rocks break the waves.

    From the waterline, a single stairway of lanterns winds up the face of the rock to the top of the arch. From the sea floor, it looks like a thread of lights climbing toward the moon. That stairway is the whole level: each landing on the way up is one of the Mob Rush arenas.

    A silhouette piece. Keep the fortress, the rock and the spire nearly black against the moon, with only small warm window and lantern lights. The palette is deep violet, dusty amber and the pale moon, with cold teal on the wet rocks and the sea below. The first wide shot should show the whole arch, the stair of lights and the spire against the moon, so players see the climb before they start it.
    Permanent night ties into the day/night system: the fortress is a time lock set to night. Inside a Sunstone pool, the only daylight in the fortress, Varyon's magic is weakest.

    The Mob Rush

    The fortress isn't a puzzle temple. It's a climb up the lantern stair, broken by a run of arenas on its landings, each built to test one of Liora's items under pressure. Between arenas, the stair hugs the outside of the rock with the sea far below and the moon getting bigger. Every arena's exit is sealed by a ward of dark crystal. When the last enemy falls, the ward cracks, and Thalos's Divine Dive breaks through it: hover Thalos with LT, hold RT to charge a glowing dive, then release it at the ward.

    1. The Mire Pit. A sunken arena flooded with knee-deep mud. Mirelings and rat soldiers come in waves. Without the Mud Boots, Liora would be swamped; with them, she can roll and dodge as normal. Rat soldiers still need Thalos to dive behind their shields.
    2. The Ice Wall. A tall shaft with ice walls. Frigidu climb the walls with Liora, and she fights while hanging on the Ice Picks, with stamina draining. Rest ledges are few. Icicles fall.
    3. The Iron Yard. Enemies in full iron armor. The sword barely scratches them, but the Magnetic Gauntlet can rip their armor off, pull them into each other, or yank their shields away. Magnetic floor traps pull Liora toward spikes unless she anchors herself to a post.
    4. The Sand Flood. The arena floor floods with flowing sand every 20 seconds. Raise Sand Rod pillars and stand on them as each flood passes, then fight Dustros and scarabs on the floor in between. Watch for Dustros grabbing Liora while she's on a pillar.
    5. The Shadow Chapel. Myrkan wraiths and Vorrik skeletons attack together. Keep a pool of daylight at Liora's feet to burn the Vorrik bones, while Thalos carries the Moonstone over the wraiths to make them solid for the Spectral Chain.
    Totem hearts and the firefly lantern's healing upgrade (20 fireflies) make a big difference here. This is a good point to remind players about collectibles before they go in.

    The throne room

    The last flight of stairs spirals up the outside of the spire to a narrow platform at its tip, with nothing above but the moon. Varyon is waiting on a throne made of a ship's prow. The Obsidian Heart sits in a stand beside him, and on a cushion, the Crown of Wrath: a jagged black crown with two of its three settings glowing purple with Thalos's pieces.

    Varyon is delighted. Liora has brought him the last piece he needs. Before she can do anything, he raises his hand and the pendant tears itself off her neck and flies to him. He sets the piece into the crown, places the Heart at its center, and puts it on. The sky goes black.

    Thalos is cut off. For a moment Liora is alone. Then the pendant, lying where Varyon dropped it, glows again. Thalos still has his voice, and Liora still has the glove.

    The duel. Varyon fights with a long black blade, sword to sword. He parries Liora's frontal attacks like Wren with the shield in Chapter 1, so lock on, sidestep, and strike from the side, or send Thalos to dive behind him and break his guard. Every few exchanges, he steps back and splits into shadow copies; put on the Mirage Mask to find the real one. It's the only phase he gets. Varyon is a man, and this is a fight between two people with swords.

    The end of it. At the end of his health bar, Varyon goes down on one knee and the Crown slips sideways on his head. He reaches up to steady it and calls on the Heart for more, the way he always has. This time something answers.

    Cutscene: the door

    Varyon's shadow on the platform stops moving when he does. Then it stands up without him. A long, thin arm of darkness rises out of it, takes Varyon by the throat and lifts him off his feet. He looks up at it the way you'd look at an old friend. "You promised. Averra would live again. I did all of this for—" Nyrosis's voice, very close and very old: "You opened the door. That is all you were for." The camera stays on their silhouette against the moon. One sharp crack, and Varyon's body drops onto the platform.

    The Crown falls from his head, hits the stone and splits. The three pieces of Thalos fly out of it and pour into the pendant on Liora's glove, and for the first time Thalos is whole. He doesn't grow. He holds himself to owl size on her arm, every feather standing up. "I am whole, Liora. And so is he."

    The Obsidian Heart, lying in the pieces of the Crown, opens like an eye.

    Thalos's three pieces weren't just hidden in their temples. They were the locks. When Thalos stood in front of Nyrosis's last blow and broke, the divines used the three pieces to seal the Heart from three places at once, so it could never open while they were apart. The rot in the temples was Nyrosis reaching through the Heart, and Varyon's cult was how he did it: he whispered Averra's rescue to a desperate man, and Varyon spent his life bringing the locks together into one Crown around the Heart. Varyon thought he was using the Heart. The Heart was using him. That's what Thalos felt on the road out of Silverspring: "something reaching through me, toward something we buried."

    Staging the death: keep it in silhouette, cut on the crack, and show no injury. It reads as more frightening that way and keeps the age rating friendly.

    Nyrosis was the Divine of Night before he fell, and every shape that hunts in the dark is his. He comes out of the Heart as a mass of shadow and pours into one form after another. Each form has its own health bar, and each one tests a different set of Liora's items. Thalos fights at full strength the whole time, since he's whole now.

    Phase 1: the forms.

    • The raven. His first form and his own, a raven as big as a ship with feathers like broken glass. He circles the spire and dives at Liora, and throws volleys of feather shards. A perfect parry knocks a dive back into him. Then it's owl against raven: hold RB and take Thalos up after him, and a Gale Wings blast while hovering knocks him out of the air onto the platform, where the sword can reach him. This is the moment the whole game has been drawing toward, one bird against the other.
    • The spider. He drops below the platform and comes back up the spire as a giant spider, and webs the platform's edges out over the drop. Webbing roots Liora where she stands. The Sunstone's daylight burns webs away, from the floor and from Liora. The spider hangs from its web above the platform, out of reach; chain it with the Spectral Chain and drag it down, then hit the soft glowing mark on its back.
    • The snake. The platform floor turns to a pool of liquid shadow, and he swims under it as a huge black snake, surfacing to strike. The Mud Boots keep Liora moving through the shadow. The Mirage Mask shows which ripple is really him and which are decoys. Raise a Sand Rod pillar right under the real one and it flings him up out of the floor, open for a combo.
    • The puma. The fastest form, and the most like Varyon's duel. He pounces from the spire wall, which the night has frozen over. Parry the pounce for an opening. When he leaps up onto the ice, follow on the Ice Picks and fight him hanging from the wall, and the Magnetic Gauntlet pulls Liora between the iron rings around the spire to cut him off.

    Phase 2: losing his grip. When the puma falls, Nyrosis can't hold a shape anymore. Every form he's worn comes back at once and won't fit together: raven wings, spider legs, the snake's body, the puma's head and jaws, with Varyon's black blade stuck through it like a spine. It's half in the spirit world and half in this one, flickering between them, and the parts that are in the spirit world can't be touched.

    • Two halves, two fighters. The physical half attacks Liora. The spirit half can only be reached by Thalos: a divine can cross into the spirit world, and now that he's whole, he can. Hold RB and fly Thalos into the flicker, and the world turns pale and inside out around him. In there, the Heart is visible at the creature's core.
    • The catch. While Liora stands still flying Thalos, she's open, and a hit on her pulls him straight back. So first chain the physical half with the Spectral Chain to pin it to the platform. Then fly Thalos into the spirit world before it breaks free, carrying the Moonstone, which makes the spirit half solid around him. Hover, hold RT to charge a Divine Dive, and release it at the Heart.
    • Three strikes on the Heart.

    The last blow. On the third strike, Nyrosis pulls everything he has left into one shape: the shadow from the Ancient Era. He raises the same blow that split the world. Thalos is back on the glove. "This is the blow. Last time I stood in front of it alone." The game takes control away for one thing only: the block prompt. Hold Triangle, and Liora raises her guard, the sword braced flat against the glove with Thalos on it, the way she's blocked all game. Press it again as the blow lands for a perfect parry. Liora and Thalos stand in front of it together, and this time nothing breaks. The blow goes back the way it came, and drives Nyrosis into the Heart.

    The parry callback is the point. In Chapter 1 Thalos told Liora that strength is "not the arm, the standing". He broke because he stood in front of that blow alone. The last thing the player does in the game is stand in front of it with him.

    The ending

    The Heart goes dark and still. Thalos lifts off Liora's arm, and grows, and grows, into the huge owl from her first vision, no longer cracked. The light spreads out over Hatu, and the permanent night over the fortress breaks for the first time into dawn.

    How does it end? Nyrosis is sealed again, and this time the seal held without breaking Thalos. Still to decide: does Thalos stay with Liora, or return to the divines? What happens to the Heart: does it stay with Liora, go back to a temple, or go into the sea? Does anyone bury Varyon, and does Averra hear what happened? Does Silverspring rebuild, and does the Consumption fade?

    The Reed Delta

    North of the Council ruins, the river Silver comes down out of the mountain and splits into dozens of shallow channels between islands of reeds. It's half land and half water, loud with frogs, and the light goes green under the willows. Every way to the swamp and the mountain starts here, and it's the first place in the game that isn't a straight road.

    • Getting across. The Rocklets laid lines of flat stepping stones across the channels long ago. They sink slowly under Liora's weight, so she has to keep moving, and a stone she stops on goes under and comes back up a few seconds later. Where a channel is too wide for stones, a plank lies on the far bank; send Thalos to carry it across and drop it into the notches. It's the first time Thalos carries something useful outside a tutorial.
    • Mother Firefly's tree stands on the first bend of the river, where the road from the fields enters the delta. This is where Chapter 2 really starts.
    • Rovans are seen here first, roosting in the dead willows and diving at Liora on the stepping stones, which is more dangerous than it sounds when the stone is sinking.
    • Two ways out. East, the channels thicken into mud, and the boardwalk into the Great Swamp begins. North, the river comes down out of a canyon: the Gorge. Its foot is a grey quicksand marsh that stops anyone without the Mud Boots.
    After Chapter 4, the Rocklets moor a raft at the delta's west end. Step on and it rides the river all the way down to Silverspring's bay in one go, past the fields and under the old bridges, a one-way shortcut home. With the Mud Boots, a quicksand pocket on the delta's east side opens to a little island with something on it (a good spot for a Totem or a Firefly).

    The Gorge

    The river's canyon, climbing from the delta up into the snow. At the bottom it's green and loud, with the river crashing over rocks. Halfway up, the spray off the waterfalls has frozen on the walls. At the top, the canyon opens onto the shelf where Emberwick sits, and the blizzard closes in.

    • The foot. The quicksand marsh, crossed with the Mud Boots, and the old stone gatehouse past it, whose wind wheel only turns with Thalos's wing blast. The full scene is in Chapter 4.
    • The ledge trail. A path cut into the canyon wall beside the river, climbing past three waterfalls. At one point the trail is broken; a dead tree leans out over the gap from above, and a wing blast knocks it down as a bridge.
    • Frigidu show up partway up, where the snow starts. The first ones are on ice, so players learn the hard way that they rebuild their armor, and the bare rock patches along the trail teach the answer.
    One of the upper waterfalls is frozen solid into a wall of blue ice, with something glowing behind it. With the Ice Picks, climb the frozen fall to a ledge behind the water and a small cave: Mystic Firefly #22. Players see it on the way up in Chapter 4, before they have the picks.

    The High Pass

    The Frostmurians' old hunting road, running east from Glaciora along the mountain's shoulder above the treeline, and then down into the Eldergrove valley from above. Liora can see it from Glaciora's eastern gate in Chapter 5, ending in a black haze far below, but she can't use it yet.

    • The broken bridge. Just past the gate, an iron bridge over a crevasse has collapsed and hangs down the far side. The Frostmurians lost it years ago. After Chapter 6, the Magnetic Gauntlet pulls it back up and locks it.
    • The wind. Gusts sweep the narrow ledges on a rhythm and shove Liora sideways. Thalos hovers in the rock shelters along the way and calls out when a gust is coming, and wing-blasts drifted snow off the path ahead.
    • The Tupilak. Its tracks lead out onto the High Pass at night, so the side quest from Glaciora runs here.
    • The far end drops into the dead forest above Eldergrove, and the Heart's mist closes it. With the Sunstone, it's a second way into Chapter 11, and afterward the fastest way between the mountain and Eldergrove.

    The Badlands

    South of the fields, the grass gives out and the ground turns to red rock, cut into canyons by a dry riverbed. The river Silver used to reach this far, long ago, which is the first hint of how the land has changed. The canyons branch and dead-end, and a heat haze blurs the distance, so it's easy to walk a long way down the wrong one.

    • Getting across. Most forks dead-end within a minute. The real way is the one the dry riverbed follows; players who notice the smooth stones and old water marks on the canyon walls can always find it. At the far end, the sandstorm wraps the deep desert, and only Selene's map leads through it.
    • Dustros are seen here first: one bursts out of the sand in a dead end, so players meet the grab-and-peck fight long before the desert.
    • The red cliff stands over one of the canyons, too soft to climb.
    Through the Mirage Mask, one dead-end wall is fake. Behind it is a little shrine with Mystic Firefly #23. With the Sand Rod, pillars climb the red cliff to Totem of Strength #14.

    The Dry Canyon

    The same dry riverbed, followed upstream from the desert's northern edge. It climbs north-east toward Eldergrove, and the closer it gets, the greyer the walls and the deader the scrub. The top is closed by the Heart's mist.

    • Getting across. With the Sunstone, burn back the mist at the top the same way as on the old road. It's how players coming straight from the Desert Temple in Chapter 10 can reach Eldergrove without going back to the fields.
    • Vorrik are seen here first, outside Eldergrove: a few skeletons in the riverbed near the top, where the Heart's leak has reached.
    • Moon doorways. The canyon walls have old priests' carvings that only open inside a pool of night, leading to small caves. Good homes for later collectibles.

    The Shore

    The coast south of Silverspring: cliff paths, coves and little beaches, with the sea crashing on rocks below. A rocky point a few minutes south of town is where the strait crossing to Selene's Island starts.

    • Tidecrabs live on the beaches and come out of the surf at low tide. They're too heavy for Thalos to flip until he has Talon Grip, so early on they're something to roll around.
    • The seabed road. With the Seashell Necklace, the sea floor is a road. From Selene's Island, walk south along the bottom past the founders' wreck and old cargo, and come ashore on the desert's beach, below the dunes. It's a second way into Chapter 9; the sandstorm still wraps the deep desert from that side, so Selene's map is still needed.
    The founders' wreck lies just off the coast, only reachable with the water parted. A good home for lore and a later collectible.
    What else lives in the in-between areas? Each one has a first-seen enemy and one secret. They could also each hold one small side character: a Rocklet ferryman in the delta, a Frostmurian hunter on the High Pass, a lost robot in the Badlands, a beachcomber on the Shore.

    Story of Hatu

    The history behind the game, from your design doc.

    Ancient Era

    The divines create the world as one continent, Averatu. One divine falls and becomes the Shadow Titan Nyrosis. The others descend to fight him and seal him in a gem, the Obsidian Heart. The battle splits the world into two continents, Averra and Hatu, and shatters Thalos into three pieces.

    Afterward, the divines decide that acting directly does more harm than good. They craft the Divine Pendants so chosen mortals can channel them instead. They build four temples on Hatu, three for the pieces of Thalos and one for the Heart, and move their faithful humans to Averra. The Rocklets keep the swamp temple, the Frostmurians the mountain temple, and the Priests of Thalos the Eldergrove temple. The desert temple is hidden where no one would go.

    First Age

    The Priests of Thalos build the village of Eldergrove around their temple. The Frostmurians and Rocklets grow their own cultures. Averra lives in peace.

    Second Age

    On Averra, government overreach leads to civil war. Greed, overuse of the land and forgotten faith bring climate change and disasters. A small faithful group takes the Divine Pendants and sails away. They wreck on Hatu's coast and found Silverspring. Selene, an explorer the village shuns, finds the desert temple and helps the robots who crash-land beside it.

    Third Age

    Hatu's peoples form the Council of Guardians to protect the land, but it slowly falls apart over old grudges and different priorities.

    Modern Age

    Varyon, a man from war-scarred Averra, joins a dark cult and reaches out to the Obsidian Heart for power. He learns of Hatu and the Crown of Wrath, which can drain Hatu's essence to rebuild Averra. The Heart's growing power destroys Eldergrove. In Silverspring, monsters gather at the edge of town, and the Pendant of Thalos chooses Liora.
