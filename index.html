    Open the world map

    The game opens on Silverspring at dawn. Fishing boats are heading out across the bay, and a narrator tells how the village was founded: a faithful few fled the wars of Averra, wrecked their ship on these cliffs, and took the spring, the fish and the wreck as a sign from the divines. The camera drifts up through the village to the watchtower just as its horn sounds. Monsters have been seen at the treeline again.

    Silverspring at a glance

    • The docks run along the bay to the south. Fishers, boat builders and a net-mender live here.
    • The market square is in the middle of the village, around the spring itself.
    • The sacred temple stands at the center of town, beside the square. It holds the Divine Pendants.
    • The Elder's house is on the hill to the north, above everything else.
    • The training yard is by the east gate, near the treeline, where the monster sightings have been.
    • The old man's hut sits outside the east gate, on the road to the Great Swamp.

    Fighter training

    Liora starts in the training yard with a wooden practice sword and three hearts. The watch captain runs the young fighters through drills. Each drill is short, and the captain only moves on once you've done it right.

    1. Basic swings. Three straw dummies stand in a row. Press attack to swing; pressing it three times in rhythm chains a three-hit combo (a slash, a backhand slash, then a heavier overhead chop). Knock the head off each dummy.
    2. The spin attack. The captain sets up a ring of five dummies around Liora. Hold attack to charge the sword (it glows and hums), then release to spin and hit everything around her. Clearing the ring in one spin ends the drill.
    3. Lock-on. A dummy swings on a rope from the crossbar. Lock on to it: the camera holds it in frame, Liora faces it and walks in a circle around it instead of running away. While locked on, pressing attack while pushing toward the target makes a forward thrust. Hit the swinging dummy three times.
    4. Dodging. The captain swings a long padded pole in wide sweeps. While locked on, jump sidesteps left or right and hops backward; when you're not locked on, it's a dodge roll. Get past three sweeps without being hit, then a quick jab from the captain teaches the backhop.
    5. The sparring match. A fellow trainee with a wooden shield squares up. Hitting the front of the shield just clangs off. The trick is to lock on, sidestep around, and strike from the side or behind. Landing three hits wins. This teaches the idea that comes back with the rat soldiers in the first temple: not every enemy can be hit from the front.

    The first real fight Proposed

    The tower horn sounds again, closer. Three Mirelings, low mud creatures with stubby legs and a glowing crack down their backs, crawl over the training yard fence. They charge in straight lines and can't turn mid-charge, so the move is to sidestep or roll as one charges, then hit it while it skids to a stop. Each takes three hits. Small Goins (the currency of Hatu) and the odd heart pop out of them, which introduces pickups.

    When the last Mireling dissolves, the captain hands Liora a real sword, a plain steel blade from the village forge. It replaces the practice sword and does double the damage.

    Sword. Liora's steel sword. Three-hit combo, spin attack (hold), and a thrust while locked on.
    Does the sword get upgrades, and does Liora get a shield? A shield would add blocking, but the dodge-and-sidestep moves already do a lot of that work, and Thalos fills the "get past a guard" role. A sword upgrade could come from the robots in the desert, or from the Frostmurian forge.

    The Elder's summons

    A messenger, a breathless kid from the market, runs into the yard. The Elder wants Liora at his house, now. The way up the hill is open to explore: villagers chat about the monsters, the net-mender has rumors, and a man in the market square is stuck outside his own front door (the Locked Out side quest, which opens once Liora has the Falconry Glove).

    At the Elder's house, he explains that for nine nights in a row, while praying to the divines, he has heard one name: Liora's. That means the divines are asking her to take up a pendant and become a Pendant Holder. He walks her down to the sacred temple himself.

    The pendant ceremony

    The sacred temple is a round hall with a ring of stone pedestals. On each one rests a pendant carved with a divine's symbol: a wave, a sheaf of grain, a flame, and others. In the middle stands the largest pedestal, holding a pendant carved with an owl. The elders gather at the walls.

    The player walks Liora around the ring. As she passes each pendant, it glows faintly and goes dark again. When she reaches the center, the owl pendant lifts off its pedestal on its own and floats into her hands. The elders go silent. The Elder says Thalos's pendant has only ever chosen one bearer before.

    Holding the pendant pulls Liora into a vision. She stands on a dark plain in front of a huge owl made of faint purple light. He is cracked, and three dim spots on his body flicker like dying coals. This is Thalos, the Divine of Strength. He tells her his body was broken into three pieces long ago and sealed in temples across Hatu. Something has gotten into those temples and is making him sick. The creatures in them are like a disease in his body, draining him. He's too weak to come into the world on his own. He asks Liora to free his temples, and tells her that the man who lives past the east gate keeps something that will let him help her.

    Who was the previous Pendant Holder? Only one person has carried Thalos's pendant before. Their story could connect to the old man, to Selene, to the war on Averra, or to whoever left the note in Liora's house in Chapter 7.

    The old man's hut

    Past the east gate, on the edge of the swamp road, is a small hut with a fenced yard and a goat. The old man inside recognizes the pendant the moment Liora walks in. He opens a chest and unwraps a worn leather falconer's glove with a purple stone set into its cuff. It belonged to the last Pendant Holder. The glove channels Thalos's power so he can take the form of a purple owl in the physical world.

    Falconry Glove. Summon Thalos and control him. Tap the Thalos button to send him to a target; hold it to fly him yourself; tap again to call him back.

    The old man walks Liora through the glove behind his hut:

    1. Summon. Raise the glove and Thalos appears in a burst of purple feathers, landing on Liora's arm.
    2. Send. A small bell hangs under the roof's eave with an owl carved above it. Lock on to it and tap the Thalos button: he flies up, rings it, and comes back. The old man points out the carving. Owl carvings always mark something Thalos can use.
    3. Fly. A target post stands across the stream, behind a fence. Hold the Thalos button to take control of his flight. Steer through the gap in the fence, flap to climb over the hedge, and dive into the target to strike it.
    4. Grab. A basket of apples sits on a high shelf on the hut's wall. Fly Thalos up and grab one, then drop it to Liora. Apples heal a quarter heart.
    5. Distract. The goat is standing in the yard gate and won't move. Send Thalos to swoop past its head: the goat chases him around the yard, and the gate is clear. The same trick works on enemies.

    Side quest: Locked Out Proposed

    Back in the market square, the man stuck outside his house is pacing. "Arghhh, I'm locked out! If I could get up on the roof and through the chimney, I could get in." Liora can't climb up there, but Thalos can fly. Hold the Thalos button and fly him up onto the roof and down the chimney. The camera follows him into the dark house. Fly to the front door and dive into the latch to knock it open. The man rushes in and comes back out with a small owl statue he found while fishing years ago, and gives it to Liora as thanks.

    Reward for Locked Out. Four Totems of Strength add one heart to Liora's health.

    Leaving Silverspring

    When Liora reaches the east gate, Thalos speaks through the pendant. He can feel one piece of himself to the east, in the Temple of Reedtona in the Great Swamp, and it's the nearest. The watch captain sees her off at the gate. The swamp road starts just past the old man's hut.

    The swamp road leaves Silverspring heading east. Within a few minutes, the dirt road turns to a rotting boardwalk, the trees close in, and the light turns green. The Great Swamp is the first real region of the game, and it's built to show players two things they can't handle yet: deep mud and quicksand.

    Getting around the swamp

    • Deep mud covers the low ground between the boardwalks. Liora can wade through it, but she slows to a crawl and can't roll. The Mud Boots fix this later.
    • Quicksand pits are marked by bubbling grey patches and the odd skull on a stick. If Liora steps in, she sinks, loses half a heart, and reappears at the edge. For now they're walls.
    • Rovans roost on dead trees. They're lanky, long-beaked swamp birds that swoop at Liora in a straight dive, then land to peck at any Goins on the ground. Roll out of the dive and hit them while they're on the ground. Two hits each.
    • Mirelings crawl out of the mud near the boardwalk edges, and they're more common the deeper into the swamp Liora goes.

    Mother Firefly

    Near the swamp's entrance, a giant cypress tree glows from inside. In a hollow at its base lives Mother Firefly, a firefly the size of a cat whose abdomen glows like a lantern. She's lonely. Her 25 children have all flown off to see the world, and she wants a family reunion. She hands Liora a small glass lantern and asks her to bring them home.

    Firefly Lantern. Holds Mystic Fireflies. When one is nearby, the lantern pulses; walk close and it drifts inside. Bring them back to Mother Firefly; she upgrades the lantern for every 5.

    As soon as Liora has the lantern, it starts pulsing. One of Mother Firefly's children is hiding in the reeds right beside the tree, as a tutorial catch.

    In the reeds beside Mother Firefly's tree.

    Past the cypress, the boardwalk splits. The path north leads to the Rocklet village and the temple. The path west leads to a huge drooping tree, where something is crying.

    The hanging bean pod Proposed

    Hanging from the lowest branch of the drooping tree is a bean pod creature: a long green pod with a sleepy face, dangling by its stem. A storm blew its three seeds out of its pod and up into the tree, and it can't let go of the branch to fetch them. If its seeds don't get planted soon, they won't grow. This is the game's first real Thalos puzzle outside a tutorial, and each seed teaches something different:

    1. The easy seed sits on a low branch with an owl carving on the bark beside it. Lock on and tap the Thalos button. He grabs it and brings it straight back.
    2. The guarded seed is in a Rovan nest on a middle branch. If Thalos goes near, the Rovan chases him off. Throw a stone or swing at the trunk to get the Rovan's attention so it dives at Liora, and while it's on the ground, send Thalos in for the seed.
    3. The high seed is caught in the top of the canopy, through a gap between branches. No carving marks it, so Liora has to hold the Thalos button and fly him there herself, flapping to climb and threading the gap.

    Once all three are back, the bean pod drops them into the mud below. Within seconds, three bean stalks shoot up, forming a living staircase up into the tree. At the top is a firefly, and the bean pod's thanks.

    Reward for the bean pod's quest. It was tangled in the roots of the tree.

    The Rocklet village

    The northern boardwalk ends at a cluster of mossy boulders rising out of the bog. These are the homes of the Rocklets: small, round stone people with moss on their heads and quiet voices. The divines put them in charge of the swamp temple long ago. They live communally, sharing everything, and care for the swamp around them.

    When Liora arrives, the village is empty. The Rocklets are hiding, curled up to look like ordinary rocks, because Rovans have taken over their sacred standing stone in the middle of the village and built nests all over it. The Rocklet elder unrolls just long enough to explain: no one goes into Thalos's temple who hasn't proven they serve Thalos. If Liora drives the Rovans off their sacred stone, they'll believe her.

    The Rocklets' request Proposed

    Three Rovan nests sit on the standing stone, too high for Liora to reach. The fight works in two layers at once:

    • While Liora fights the Rovans swooping down at her on the ground, she sends Thalos up to knock nests off the stone. Each nest has an owl carving glowing faintly behind it.
    • Every nest knocked down makes the rest of the Rovans angrier, so more dive at once.

    When the third nest falls, the Rovan Alpha lands on the stone. It's twice the size of the others, with a ragged crest. It flies in a circle overhead and then dives at Liora in a long, fast arc. If she rolls out of the way at the last second, it slams beak-first into the mud and gets stuck for a few seconds. That's the window to hit it. Three stuck-in-the-mud hits finish it.

    The Rocklets unroll and cheer. They also have news. Days ago, a hooded stranger walked through their village toward the temple without a word, and since then the temple has been groaning at night. The elder leads Liora to the edge of the village, where a moat of black water surrounds the temple island. A dozen Rocklets roll into the water, line up, and become a stepping-stone bridge to the temple door.

    Inside a hollow stump at the edge of the Rocklet village. The opening is too small for Liora; send Thalos in.
    The snail cartographer is resting on a lily pad just off the Rocklet village boardwalk. He sells a map of the Great Swamp.
    The hooded stranger is the first hint of Varyon. Players won't know yet, but this explains why the temple's piece of Thalos is already missing at the end of Chapter 3.

    1Sunken Gate R1

    The entrance is a half-flooded stone archway at the base of the tree. Inside is a short hall with an iron portcullis blocking the way. There's no switch anywhere Liora can reach.

    Look up and to the right of the portcullis. High on the wall is a small alcove behind a narrow gap in the stone, and above the gap is an owl carving. Inside the alcove is a round stone switch. Hold the Thalos button to fly, steer him up through the gap, and dive into the switch. The portcullis rattles up.

    This is the same move as the target post behind the old man's hut, but with a tighter gap. Flap once to gain height before you line up with it.

    2Great Hollow R2

    The temple's central room is the hollow inside the tree's trunk. It's huge, dim and round, with roots as thick as columns curving up the walls and a ring walkway around the edge. At the bottom is a shallow, still pool.

    • West: an open doorway to the Lantern Gallery.
    • East: a door with a small keyhole.
    • North: a wide pit of quicksand. High above it, on a ledge of roots, is a huge door wrapped in iron bands with a large keyhole: the boss door.

    If Liora tries the quicksand, she sinks and comes back out at the edge. Thalos can fly over it and look at the boss door up close, and if you do, he says quietly that his piece is beyond it. There's no way across yet, so head west.

    3Lantern Gallery R3

    This long hall is pitch black except for one brazier burning just inside the door. The Firefly Lantern lights a small circle around Liora, enough to see that the floor ends a few steps in. A chasm cuts the room in half. Across it, three dead braziers sit on stone pillars at different heights, each with an owl carving.

    1. First brazier. Send Thalos to the lit brazier by the door. He grabs a burning stick from it. Now fly him across the chasm to the nearest dead brazier and drop the stick in. It flares up, and a section of stone bridge grinds up out of the chasm.
    2. Second brazier. This one is hidden behind a curtain of hanging moss. Fly Thalos to the burning brazier for a new stick, then into the moss. The moss catches and burns away, and the stick lights the brazier behind it. A second bridge section rises.
    3. Third brazier. The last one is at the far end, past a low wooden beam. The burning stick only lasts about eight seconds before it goes out, which isn't quite long enough to fly the whole way. There's a wall sconce halfway along. Light the sconce with the first stick, then grab a fresh stick from the sconce to finish the trip, ducking under the beam. The last bridge section rises.

    Cross the bridge. Three mud rats scurry out of a crack to meet Liora; two sword hits each. A chest at the end holds the first key.

    Small Key 1

    Now that the room is lit, the lantern pulses near the back wall. A firefly has been hiding in a nook behind the third brazier, where the dark kept it hidden.

    4Rat Barracks R4

    Go back to the Great Hollow and unlock the east door. The room beyond looks like old sleeping quarters, with rotted bunk beds along the walls and a drain grate in the floor on the north side.

    A dozen ordinary rats scurry around the bunks. When Liora gets close, four of them stand up and grow into rat soldiers: man-sized, hunched, holding round wooden shields. They block every sword hit from the front, just like the trainee in Chapter 1.

    • Dive from behind. Lock on to a soldier and tap the Thalos button. Thalos swoops around behind it and slams into its back. The soldier staggers and drops its shield arm for about two seconds. Hit it with the full combo.
    • Or distract. If Thalos lands on the floor, the soldiers turn to chase him and show Liora their backs.

    When the four are down, the Rat Captain climbs out from under the biggest bunk. It's taller, with a heavy door-sized shield, and Thalos has to dive at it twice to knock its guard down. Its swings are slow but take a full heart. When it falls, it drops a key, and a chest appears in the middle of the room.

    Look through the drain grate on the north side. There's a tunnel down there and an old wooden raft lying on the bottom, but almost no water. Remember it.

    5Lily Cistern R5

    Return to the Lantern Gallery and use the second key on its north door. The room beyond is a huge round stone reservoir, almost empty. Giant lily pads lie flat on the mud at the bottom, and a ledge with an owl statue on it runs around the very top of the room, far out of reach.

    Three sluice valves are set high in the walls, each one with an owl carving and a water drop carved beside it: one small, one medium and one large. A Rocklet mural by the entrance shows three water drops being poured into a bowl, smallest first.

    1. Send Thalos to the valve with the small drop. Water spills out and the pool rises a third of the way. The lily pads float up.
    2. Hop onto a lily pad. Send Thalos to the medium drop. The water rises again, carrying Liora up with it.
    3. Send him to the large drop. The water fills the reservoir, and the lily pads reach the top ledge.

    If you get the order wrong, all three valves slam shut, the water drains, and two mud rats crawl out of the outflow. Start again from the smallest.

    On the owl pedestal on the top ledge of the Lily Cistern.

    As the reservoir fills, a short cutscene shows water rushing through the temple's pipes into the Rat Barracks drain. The raft at the bottom of the tunnel floats up. Thalos says something moved in the barracks.

    6Storm Courtyard R6 Mini-boss

    Go back through the Great Hollow to the Rat Barracks. The drain is full of water now, and the raft is floating right under the grate. Pull the grate aside, step onto the raft, and it carries Liora along the tunnel and out into the open air.

    The Storm Courtyard is a stone yard on the outside of the tree, high above the swamp, under a thunderstorm that never stops. Rain pours down, puddles cover the stones, and lightning strikes every six seconds or so. Three copper lightning rods stand on stone towers around the yard. Each one has a copper plate at its base with an owl carving, and a ring of scorched stones around it.

    The Gloomstalker is a gaunt, long-limbed swamp creature that is completely invisible except during a lightning flash. Lock-on doesn't work on it while it's unseen.

    How to track it: watch the puddles. Wherever it steps, water splashes. When a flash hits, it shows up as a black silhouette for a moment. It swipes with long claws from behind or the side, for one heart each.

    How to hurt it: lock on to a lightning rod and tap the Thalos button. He strikes the copper plate, and lightning hits that rod within a second. The flash reveals the Gloomstalker for three seconds, and if it was standing inside that rod's ring of scorched stones, the bolt stuns it. Run in and hit it while it's stunned.

    Phase 2: after two stuns, it knocks one of the towers over, leaving only two rods. It also stops following Liora directly and circles at a distance. Stand inside a ring yourself to lure it in, then call the lightning as its splashes come close. Roll out of the ring just before the bolt lands.

    Three stuns and a few hits each finish it. It dissolves into the rain.

    When the next lightning flash hits, look at the courtyard's back wall. For a split second, a row of root steps shows up, leading to a ledge with a chest. They're always there, just impossible to see in the gloom. Climb them carefully between flashes.

    Mud Boots. Walk through deep mud at full speed and across quicksand without sinking.

    7Quicksand Moat R7

    The courtyard's west exit leads back inside to the north side of the quicksand pit Liora saw in the Great Hollow. With the Mud Boots on, she can walk right across it at full speed. The quicksand bubbles around her boots, but she doesn't sink.

    Rovans nest in the roots above the pit. They dive at Liora as she crosses, trying to knock her off course into the deepest part, where she'd slow down. Roll out of their dives and hit them when they land. On the far side, kick down a wooden gate to open a shortcut back into the Great Hollow. Then go north.

    8Sinking Shrine R8

    This long chamber is floored with mud flats. A channel along one side carries a tide of liquid mud that rises and falls on a cycle of about 20 seconds. At high tide, the mud floods the whole chamber chest-deep, too deep even with the Mud Boots, and pushes Liora back to the entrance.

    At the far end is a wooden floodgate. Its switch sits on a high perch above it, marked with an owl carving. The switch only holds the gate shut while something is pressing it.

    1. Send Thalos to the perch switch. He lands on it and stays there, holding the floodgate shut. Control snaps back to Liora on its own.
    2. Run across the mud flats in the Mud Boots. Without the boots, this would take far too long.
    3. While Thalos is perched, Rovans swoop at him. Every ten seconds or so, one knocks him off the switch and the tide starts to rise again. If that happens, re-send him from wherever Liora is.

    In the middle of the chamber, on a small island of solid stone, is a chest.

    Small Key 3

    North of the island is a big door with a heavy lock. It's the boss door, seen from the other side. It needs the boss key. The west wall has a locked door; use the third key on it.

    9Root Lift R9

    The west door opens into a tall shaft inside the tree. A wide wooden platform hangs on thick roots at the bottom, tied by a rope over a pulley to an empty basket hanging high above. The platform is the lift; the basket is its counterweight. On a ledge across the shaft is a pile of stones with an owl carving.

    Stand on the platform and send Thalos to the stone pile. He grabs a stone. Fly him to the basket and drop it in. The basket sinks a little and the platform rises one level. It takes four stones to reach the top.

    Rovans live in this shaft too. If one hits Thalos while he's carrying a stone, he drops it and has to go back for another. Weave around them, or have Liora draw them down to the platform first and clear them.

    At the top, a door leads north. There's also a ledge on the south side where Liora can drop down into the Lily Cistern. That's a one-way shortcut back toward the entrance.

    10Heartwood Vault R10

    A round chamber with a muddy floor. In the middle is a raised dais with a large chest. Around the dais runs a narrow channel of clear, clean water.

    As Liora steps toward the chest, mud rats start pulling themselves out of the floor, and they keep coming. They dissolve if they touch the clean water, though. Walk into the channel and fight from inside it: the rats have to come to Liora, and any that step into the water melt away. After the sixth one, the floor stops bubbling. Climb onto the dais and open the chest.

    Heartwood Key. A key carved from living wood. It opens the door to the Bog Guardian.

    Take the Root Lift back down, then go through the Sinking Shrine to the boss door. If the tide is up, send Thalos to the floodgate switch again.

    11Bog Guardian's Mire R11 Boss

    The boss door opens onto the very top of the tree: a wide, round arena with no roof, open to the same endless storm as the courtyard. The floor is almost all mud. At the far end is a stone pedestal shaped to hold something. It's empty.

    The mud in the middle of the arena heaves up into the Bog Guardian: a towering golem made of mud, vines and swamp plants. Deep in its chest, behind a mesh of vines, glows a core of living heartwood. A big swamp flower is knotted in the vines over the core.

    Phase 1. The Guardian slams its fists into the mud, sending out a ring-shaped shockwave. Jump or roll through it. It also scoops up mud and throws it in lobbed arcs. After two or three attacks, it sinks into the floor and moves under the mud.

    While it's under, a trail of bubbles moves through the mud. Lock on to the bubbles with Thalos to scout ahead: he flies over them and circles the spot where they're gathering, which is where it will come up. In the Mud Boots, Liora can get there first. When it rises, it's slow for a few seconds. Send Thalos at the flower over its chest. He rips the flower out, and the vines peel open, exposing the heartwood core. Hit the core with the full combo or a spin attack. Do this three times.

    Phase 2. The Guardian splits into three smaller mud mounds that circle the arena and spit mud at Liora. Only one has the core inside, and in the gloom they look the same. Watch the lightning: every flash lights up the core's glow inside one mound for a moment. Lock on to that mound and hit it before it moves. When hit, it reshuffles with the other two. Three hits on the right mound end the fight.

    The Guardian collapses back into the mud, and its core lies in the muck, clean and quiet. Thalos is a little stronger, now that the temple is free.

    Gale Wings. Thalos can now fly through strong wind without being blown back. See all scrolls.

    Liora walks to the pedestal. It's empty, and the dust on it has been disturbed recently. Thalos's voice is quiet: his piece is gone. Someone got here first. He can still feel it, though: it's been carried far to the north, toward the snowy mountain. Stepping on the owl seal behind the pedestal carries Liora back to the temple entrance.

    The Temple of Reedtona's piece of Thalos was gone before Liora ever got there. Thalos can feel it to the north, on the snowy mountain, which is also where his second temple is. Before Liora leaves the swamp, the Rocklets let her know that the hooded stranger went north, too.

    With the Mud Boots, the swamp is now easy to get around. Pockets of the swamp blocked by quicksand earlier are open now, and they're good places for future fireflies and Totems.

    The foothills

    North of the swamp, the ground rises into pine forest and then rocky foothills. Snow starts to show up on the ground, then gets deeper. Mirelings give way to Frigidu as Liora climbs: small, quick ice imps covered in plates of ice armor. They hop in and swipe with claws. A full combo shatters the armor, and two more hits finish them. If one is standing on ice or snow when its armor breaks, it rebuilds the armor from the ground in a few seconds, so knock them onto bare rock first, or kill them fast.

    The snail cartographer is at the foot of the mountain trail, where the foothills begin. He sells a map of the mountain. Notice that the map shows a small village partway up the trail, but nothing around it: just white.

    Into the blizzard Proposed

    Partway up the trail, the wind picks up. Snow starts blowing sideways, then thicker, until the trail, the trees, and finally everything but Liora is gone. The screen goes almost completely white. The music drops out to nothing but wind. Liora keeps walking, because there's nothing else to do.

    Then a warm orange light shows through the snow ahead: a window. A few more steps, and the blizzard thins into a pocket of calm. Liora has stumbled into a tiny village.

    Emberwick Proposed

    Emberwick is barely a village. It's three houses around a small snowy square, with a little cave mouth in the rock at its edge. On every side, just past the last house, the blizzard stands like a wall of white. Inside that circle, the air is still, and snow falls softly.

    In the middle of the square is a great stone lantern, taller than Liora, carved with flames and mountain spirits. It's cold and dark. A thin crust of ice covers the empty oil dish inside.

    The three houses:

    • The lamp-maker's house. An old woman who makes the lantern oil. She explains everything: the great lantern is an offering to the spirits of the peak. As long as it burns, the spirits let travelers through the storms. When it goes out, the mountain closes its doors.
    • The trapper's house. A trapper who's been stuck here for days. He tried to walk out north, walked for an hour in the whiteout, and came back into town from the south. "Every road out of here leads home," he says, and he doesn't mean it nicely.
    • The way-house. A small inn with a big hearth, run by a cheerful innkeeper who's quietly worried about running out of firewood. Liora can rest here to restore her hearts.

    The loop

    Try to leave in any direction, and the same thing happens. Walk north past the last house, and the whiteout swallows Liora. She walks, and walks, with nothing but wind and white. After about thirty seconds, shapes come out of the snow ahead: a house, a lantern. She's walking back into Emberwick, from the south. Walk east, and she comes back from the west. It works the same in every direction.

    • Thalos can try, too. Gale Wings lets him fly straight up into the storm, but the whiteout goes as high as he can climb, and he can't see the ground. He comes back to the glove. "This storm is not weather," he says. "It's a door, and it's shut."
    • Footprints show the loop. Liora's own tracks lead out of town to the north, and back in from the south.
    Each exit is a trigger zone at the edge of the whiteout. Crossing it starts a short whiteout walk (an empty, snow-filled scene with a timer), then teleports Liora to the opposite edge of town, facing inward. Because the camera never shows the cut, it reads as walking in a circle. Once the lantern is lit, one exit trigger leads to the real trail instead.

    The Abura-sumashi

    Huddled at the foot of the cold lantern is an Abura-sumashi: a short, round creature in a straw cloak, with a head like a big potato and tiny worried eyes. It's the lantern's keeper. Every night, it carries the village's flask of oil to the lantern and keeps the flame burning.

    A few nights ago, it went to fetch the oil from where it's stored, in the little cave at the edge of town, out of the cold. Someone was in there: a hooded stranger who had come out of the storm, with a fire burning. He frightened it so badly that it dropped the flask and ran. It hasn't dared go back. With no oil, the lantern went out, and the storm closed in around Emberwick.

    It won't move from the lantern, and it won't go into the cave. It asks Liora to bring back the flask.

    The cave

    The cave entrance is a crack in the rock behind the lamp-maker's house. Inside, it's dark and cold, and the Firefly Lantern's light only reaches a short way.

    1. The first chamber has a family of Frigidu living in it. There's a patch of bare rock near the entrance; fight them there so they can't rebuild their armor.
    2. The crawlway. A narrow passage leads deeper, too low for Liora until it's cleared. A rock is wedged inside, and only Thalos fits in to knock it loose from the far side. Hold the Thalos button and fly him through, then dive into the rock.
    3. The camp. The back chamber has a small camp in it. There's a bedroll, two crates, and a fire pit that's still smoldering, though nobody is here. The fire smells like lamp oil. Leaning against one of the crates is a pair of steel ice picks.
    Ice Picks. Climb walls of ice. Climbing uses stamina; rest on ledges to recover.
    Circling the smoldering fire pit at the camp. That's five; bring them back to Mother Firefly for the first lantern upgrade, which makes the lantern glow brighter.

    The oil flask isn't by the fire. Whoever camped here used some of the oil to start the fire, then left the flask on a ledge of solid ice at the back of the chamber, out of reach.     How clearly is the camp Varyon's? The hooded stranger burned the sacred lantern's oil to keep warm, which says a lot about him. Something left behind, like a scrap of a drawing of a crown, could hint at the Crown of Wrath without explaining it.

    Relighting the great lantern

    1. The way-house hearth is the only fire in town. Send Thalos to grab a burning stick from it.
    2. Fly it across the square to the lantern and drop it in.

    The homestead Proposed

    1. Climb the cliff above the cabin with the Ice Picks. It's a tall climb with two rest ledges.
    2. The roses grow out of a crack in the ice, too narrow for Liora's hand. Send Thalos in to pick them.
    3. On the way back down, Liora can look around the clearing. Near the woodshed and half-buried under the snow, there are bones, in several places, all around the house. Examine them, and Thalos says quietly that these were not left by wolves.

    Arriving in Glaciora

    The feats Proposed

    The feats take place in the village's big open square. Villagers gather to watch, and they cheer and jeer.

    1. The Climb. Liora races a young Frostmurian up the Chieftain's Wall, a tall ice wall at the back of the square, to ring the bell at the top. He climbs at a steady pace.     2. The Hunt. Carved spirit masks slide along ropes strung across the square, at different heights and speeds. Thalos grabs snowballs from a big drift and drops them on the masks. Hit 8 masks in 60 seconds. The highest masks are worth two, but Thalos has to climb to reach them.
    3. The Stone. In the center of the square is the Ancestor Stone, a boulder only chieftains are said to have lifted. Liora lifts it overhead and has to hold it there for 15 seconds.     When Liora sets the Ancestor Stone down, the square goes quiet, then erupts. The chieftain laughs and grants her the right to enter the temple. He also gives her an old owl carving from his hall.

    Side quest: Say Another One Proposed

    Sitting on a bench in the square is an enormous Frostmurian strongman, and on his shoulder is a tiny Frostmurian telling jokes. "Say another one, funny man!" the big one says. The little one tells it. The big one laughs. They've been doing this for years. (Like Gabora and his partner in Majora's Mask.)

    The trouble is that the little one has run out of jokes, and has been telling the same ten for months. The big one has started to notice. The little one begs Liora to find him some new material.

    Side quest: The Tupilak Proposed

    Gryxil falls and fades. Where it lay is a long chain of pale, glowing links with a hook at the end. It's light as air.

    2. Throw the Spectral Chain at the first lantern. It latches on. Pull, and the lantern becomes solid and drops into place as a stone platform hanging in the air.

    9Bell Tower E9

    Go back to the Nave of Bells. The ghostly staircase on the north side is a spirit, so the Spectral Chain can catch it. Throw the chain at it and pull, and the staircase drops into the world with a heavy thud, solid stone.

    The stairs lead up into the bell tower. Five bells of different sizes hang in it, at different heights. Nothing tells you here what order to ring them. The answer is on the gravestones: the epitaphs in the churchyard and on the hill each mention a bell, and together they give the order (for example, "the least shall be first," "the great one rings after the least," and so on).

    Thalos rings each bell. With Resonant Strike, he can ring them from wherever he's perched. Ring them in the right order, and the tower rumbles. A stair that was buried in the floor of the nave below starts sinking down, deeper into the temple.

    A wrong order makes the bells' sound summon two Myrkan; fight them off and try again.

    11Heart Chamber E12 Boss

    The Obsidian Seal Key opens a black glass door. Beyond it is a vast round vault. In the middle, on a pedestal, under a shaft of grey light, is the Obsidian Heart: a black gem the size of a head, pulsing slowly.

    Between Liora and the Heart, a figure in tattered priest's robes kneels in prayer. He stands and turns. It's the Hollow Archpriest, the last high priest of Thalos, who stayed with the Heart when the others fled. Its power has hollowed him out. His face is gone, there's only shadow under his hood, and his robes flicker between this world and the spirit world.

    Phase 1: the spirit realm. The Archpriest fades into the spirit world, where he can't be touched. He drifts around the vault and throws bolts of shadow. Throw the Moonstone near him: inside the pool of night, he shows up as a ghost. Throw the Spectral Chain at him, latch on, and pull him back into the physical world. He's stunned and solid. Hit him.

    Phase 2: day and night. He changes his attacks depending on the light around him. Inside a pool of daylight, he summons Vorrik skeletons and sweeps a scythe of light. Inside a pool of night, he splits into three ghost copies and throws shadow bolts. Outside both, he hides in the spirit world. Keep a pool wherever suits you, and send Thalos to carry the other stone and move its light around the vault. The real Archpriest is the one the Mirage Mask shows as solid; chain him and pull him in.

    Phase 3: the Heart. The Archpriest draws power directly from the Heart, and floats up out of reach above it. Shadow pours off the Heart in waves across the floor; jump or roll through them. Between waves, chain him and pull, and he's dragged down to the floor. While he's pinned, Thalos dives at his hood to keep him from rising, and Liora strikes. Three pulls and he falls.

    The Archpriest crumples, and just for a moment, he's a tired old man again. He thanks Liora, and fades away.

    Varyon

    Liora walks to the pedestal and reaches for the Heart, to take it before Varyon can. Thalos cries out to stop. The Heart is too strong; touching it with her bare hands would destroy her.

    And then a voice says he'd love to hold it. Varyon is standing in the doorway. He rushes past her and grabs the Heart.

    Liora throws the Spectral Chain like a whip, and it wraps around the Heart too. For a long moment, they're locked together, each pulling on the gem. Varyon is smiling. He says a single word, and a blast of dark magic throws Liora across the vault. When she looks up, he's gone, and so is the Heart.

    Thalos's voice is grim. Varyon has the Heart and two of his pieces, and he only needs the last one to complete the Crown of Wrath. The one Liora is carrying.

    Varyon has the Obsidian Heart and two pieces of Thalos. He needs the third piece, the one in Liora's pendant, to complete the Crown of Wrath. Then he can drain Hatu's essence to rebuild Averra, destroying Hatu in the process. Thalos can feel the Heart and his pieces together, out to sea.

    Reaching the fortress Proposed

    Varyon's fortress stands on a black rock off Hatu's western coast, where the sea is deepest. It was built from the hulls of Averran warships, bolted together and grown over with dark crystal. There's no bridge. Liora walks there across the sea floor with the Seashell Necklace, through the deepest water in the game. The walls of water rise so high they block out the sky, and the shape from the strait swims past again.

    Where is the fortress? Out at sea makes the Seashell Necklace part of the finale. It could also be in Averra itself, or rise out of the ground somewhere on Hatu once the Crown is nearly complete.

    The Mob Rush

    The fortress isn't a puzzle temple. It's a run of arenas, each built to test one of Liora's items under pressure. Every arena's exit is sealed by a ward of dark crystal. When the last enemy falls, the ward cracks, and Thalos's Divine Dive breaks through it: hold the Thalos button to charge a glowing dive, then release at the ward.

    1. The Mire Pit. A sunken arena flooded with knee-deep mud. Mirelings and rat soldiers come in waves. Without the Mud Boots, Liora would be swamped; with them, she can roll and dodge as normal. Rat soldiers still need Thalos to dive behind their shields.
    2. The Ice Wall. A tall shaft with ice walls. Frigidu climb the walls with Liora, and she fights while hanging on the Ice Picks, with stamina draining. Rest ledges are few. Icicles fall.
    3. The Iron Yard. Enemies in full iron armor. The sword barely scratches them, but the Magnetic Gauntlet can rip their armor off, pull them into each other, or yank their shields away. Magnetic floor traps pull Liora toward spikes unless she anchors herself to a post.
    4. The Sand Flood. The arena floor floods with flowing sand every 20 seconds. Raise Sand Rod pillars and stand on them as each flood passes, then fight Dustros and scarabs on the floor in between. Watch for Dustros grabbing Liora while she's on a pillar.
    5. The Shadow Chapel. Myrkan wraiths and Vorrik skeletons attack together. Keep a pool of daylight at Liora's feet to burn the Vorrik bones, while Thalos carries the Moonstone over the wraiths to make them solid for the Spectral Chain.
    Totem hearts and the firefly lantern's healing upgrade (20 fireflies) make a big difference here. This is a good point to remind players about collectibles before they go in.

    The throne room

    At the top of the fortress is a great hall open to the sky, where storm clouds turn in a slow spiral. Varyon is waiting on a throne made of a ship's prow. The Obsidian Heart sits in a stand beside him, and on a cushion, the Crown of Wrath: a jagged black crown with two of its three settings glowing purple with Thalos's pieces.

    Varyon is delighted. Liora has brought him the last piece he needs. Before she can do anything, he raises his hand and the pendant tears itself off her neck and flies to him. He sets the piece into the crown, places the Heart at its center, and puts it on. The sky goes black.

    Thalos is cut off. For a moment Liora is alone. Then the pendant, lying where Varyon dropped it, glows again. Thalos still has his voice, and Liora still has the glove.

    Phase 1: the duel. Varyon fights with a long black blade, sword to sword. He parries Liora's frontal attacks like the trainee with the shield in Chapter 1, so lock on, sidestep, and strike from the side, or send Thalos to dive behind him and break his guard. Every few exchanges, he steps back and splits into shadow copies; put on the Mirage Mask to find the real one.

    Phase 2: the stolen powers. Varyon calls on the powers of the temples he's drained, and each one is answered by the item from that temple:

    • He floods the floor with mud, and the Mud Boots keep Liora moving.
    • He raises ice walls and climbs them; the Ice Picks follow him up, and the Gauntlet pulls him down by his iron pauldrons.
    • He calls a sandstorm and sand waves; Sand Rod pillars ride them out.
    • He floods the hall with shadow; the Sunstone burns it back, and an eclipse with both stones leaves him exposed.

    Phase 3: the Heart. The Heart takes over. Varyon grows huge and half-shadow, and something else, something very old, looks out of his eyes. That's Nyrosis, the Shadow Titan, stirring inside the Heart. He's in the spirit world and the physical world at once. Chain him to drag him fully into the world. While he's pinned, charge a Divine Dive and send Thalos straight at the Crown of Wrath. The dive cracks it. Three cracked dives, and the Crown shatters.

    The last blow. Varyon falls to his knees, human-sized again, the Crown in pieces. Liora's sword does the rest.

    The ending

    The three pieces of Thalos rise out of the broken Crown and flow into the pendant. For the first time, Thalos is whole. He lifts off Liora's arm, and grows, and grows, into the huge owl from her first vision, but no longer cracked. The light spreads out over Hatu.

    How does it end? The doc says Liora saves Hatu. Still to decide: does Thalos stay with Liora, or return to the divines? What happens to the Heart, and is Nyrosis still sealed? What happens to Varyon? Does Silverspring rebuild, and does the Consumption fade?

    Story of Hatu

    The history behind the game, from your design doc.

    Ancient Era

    The divines create the world as one continent, Averatu. One divine falls and becomes the Shadow Titan Nyrosis. The others descend to fight him and seal him in a gem, the Obsidian Heart. The battle splits the world into two continents, Averra and Hatu, and shatters Thalos into three pieces.

    Afterward, the divines decide that acting directly does more harm than good. They craft the Divine Pendants so chosen mortals can channel them instead. They build four temples on Hatu, three for the pieces of Thalos and one for the Heart, and move their faithful humans to Averra. The Rocklets keep the swamp temple, the Frostmurians the mountain temple, and the Priests of Thalos the Eldergrove temple. The desert temple is hidden where no one would go.

    First Age

    The Priests of Thalos build the village of Eldergrove around their temple. The Frostmurians and Rocklets grow their own cultures. Averra lives in peace.

    Second Age

    On Averra, government overreach leads to civil war. Greed, overuse of the land and forgotten faith bring climate change and disasters. A small faithful group takes the Divine Pendants and sails away. They wreck on Hatu's coast and found Silverspring. Selene, an explorer the village shuns, finds the desert temple and helps the robots who crash-land beside it.

    Third Age

    Hatu's peoples form the Council of Guardians to protect the land, but it slowly falls apart over old grudges and different priorities.

    Modern Age

    Varyon, a man from war-scarred Averra, joins a dark cult and reaches out to the Obsidian Heart for power. He learns of Hatu and the Crown of Wrath, which can drain Hatu's essence to rebuild Averra. The Heart's growing power destroys Eldergrove. In Silverspring, monsters gather at the edge of town, and the Pendant of Thalos chooses Liora.
